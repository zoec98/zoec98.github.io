# Sonia packs and flies to Shanghai

Checking into GAIA, Sonia surveyed her surroundings.
She quickly identified a large number of Changed individuals, distinguished by their unique style of dress and behavior.
"Let's do some science," she thought to herself, deciding to stay in the lobby for a bit to count,
while Anna continued up to her office.
"Changed versus Unchanged, let's see the ratio."
Fifteen minutes later, she had her numbers:
approximately 15 percent of all personnel, regardless of gender, had been Changed.
She also overheard conversations about many people calling in sick, indicating the virus had infiltrated widely,
altering people she once knew and imprinting itself on their bodies and minds.
She understood she couldn't trust those unaffected by the virus; their turn would come soon.
For now, she had to find a way to fight the virus and find answers on her own,
leveraging her own immunity. 

Encumbered by the stupid Yoyah Sheengzoh and higher-than-comfortable heels,
Sonia shuffled into her small above-ground office.
She had to unbutton her blouse's cuffs partly to be able to rid herself of the gloves, 
which was a nuisance, but gloved she could hardly type, and absolutely not use her trackpad.
Finally, she was able to immerse herself in research.
She collected reports, statistical data on infection rates, spread, and R-values,
and built a timeline to pinpoint the origin of the infection.
She noticed a curious parallel development between the infection and the "Maylee Shufoo" chain of stores.
"Where the virus goes, the boutiques show up shortly after," she noted.
"Maybe that will let me put a face to the opposition.
Unlike viruses, shops tend to have names attached to them.
I'll find out who owns these things."

Corporate financial structures are different from genome data, but Sonia was deeply motivated.
She checked LinkedIn and found few clues, except for Celine Durmont,
listed as a boutique regional manager with skills in "Corporate Restructuring"
and "Asset Protection," and links to the consulting firm "Drexel and Saigne LLC."
The Geneva Chamber of Commerce registrations for Maylee Shufoo boutiques in Geneva led her to Veridis Quo,
a holding listing one Maximilian Grothe,
but that was a dead end.
Maximilian Grothe had no findable public records, connections, or personal details.

"Okay, I have found some things, but I am truly out of my depth here," she admitted to herself.
"I need professional help.
Very professional help."
In her mind, she went through a list of people that might be better suited than herself in trying to solve that.
She decided to call Mara Delacroix, a high-powered independent corporate lawyer that GAIA usually worked with.
Mara had handled contracts for GAIA concerning the acquisition of genome data and research contracts,
and worked on legal policies for handling medical and other sensitive personal data.
She wasn't exactly a friend, Sonia wondered if "Mara, the Merciless" had any friends at all,
but if anybody would get her results it would be her.

Since she was on a legal consulting contract with GAIA, getting Mara's help was straightforward.
They met for lunch at a small eatery around the corner from GAIA.
That meant Sonia had to deal with the nuisance of her gloves and too many buttons again, twice,
unless she wanted to incur the wrath of any Changed that saw her.
Mara, a small, almost waifish figure, and visibly Unchanged,
showed up dressed in a sharp business suit with almost no jewelry.
She was obviously in a hurry.
"Sonia, sorry for being late," Mara apologized.
"But I managed to squeeze you in for a quick lunch; I've been nearby," she explained.
"Thanks for coming, Mara."
They ordered sandwiches, some fruit juice, and a hot cocoa for Sonia.

"No coffee?" Mara inquired.
"Have you ever met Anna?" Sonia replied, prompting a smile from Mara. 
"I don't know what exactly she does to her stuff, but if you've ever had that,
you're a lost cause for coffee from places like this."

Sonia explained her problem in a careful, non-specific way.
"There is this new chain of shops that have been popping up all around Geneva.
I… GAIA… we have reason to research them and find out the true owner of this chain.
You may have seen them, Chinese-themed, Maylee Shufoo," she began.
"Oh, right," Mara answered.
"The Stepford wives/bondage shop crossover chain?
Why would a genome research institute…" Sonia smiled thinly.
"Right," Mara stopped herself.
"I don't actually need an explanation.
I'll call you when I have found something.
Anyway, this chain is weird,
have you had a look…" Only then did Mara realize that Sonia was also wearing some Maylee Shufoo.
"Wait.
Why are you wearing their stuff?
This is totally not your style.
I mean, you can dress any way you want, but I would have never pegged you for… Sorry."
Mara caught herself.

"It's fine.
And you're right.
It is not my style," Sonia responded.
"But there's a strange connection between Maylee Shufoo and a specific viral infection that I,
I mean GAIA, needs to investigate."
Sonia paused, reflecting.
"Maylee Shufoo is quite popular, and apparently, I need to fit in, being a field researcher and all."
It was a flimsy excuse, but it seemed sufficient for Mara, who was already mentally preparing for her next meeting.

Involving Mara turned out to be one of Sonia's better decisions.
Within just a day, Mara efficiently navigated what had seemed a daunting task to Sonia.
The next morning, Sonia found a wealth of legal documents in her inbox:
scans of corporate registrations, shell companies, annual and quarterly reports—all the essentials.
The trails of evidence from Drexel and Saigne LLC and Veridis Quo Holdings pointed to a network of overseas ownership structures
that included another boutique chain,
迷人的景象 or "Mirende Ginciao,"
which Sonia automatically translated to "A Captivating Sight."
Known colloquially in the US market as "Mirren's,"
the chain also had a few outlets in some European regions and offered products very similar to Maylee Shufoo.
The intricate corporate web pointed back to Shanghai, aligning with the Chinese theme of both chains.

Sonia had previously visited Shanghai on field trips and had friends and contacts there.
Armed with the information from Mara, she sent out several emails seeking further details.
The responses were mostly fruitless: 
Zhou Wei, a journalist she knew, 
could provide no additional information and asked more questions than she was prepared to answer.
Wang Jie, a tech entrepreneur, explored his local tech and network scenes,
but his connections to the biosciences were too weak to be of any use in this context.

Ultimately, it was Jiang Han, known as "The Broker," who had the information she needed,
provided Sonia didn't inquire about how it was obtained.
"It's strange.
All leads point to Shanghai, and now to a single person.
It's almost like a cliché Bond villain scenario,"
she mused as she sifted through the dossier, a collection of PDFs on her workstation.

Dr. Mingyu Cheng,
an American-born Chinese man who had made his fortune in biotech—Sonia's own field—founded 
Cheng Biotech Industries (CBI) in '96,
aiming to revolutionize treatments for neurological disorders.
Sonia was well aware of the company, and suddenly the virus seemed like a blatantly obvious scheme.
"Treatment of neurological disorders, my ass," she scoffed, her laugh hollow.
Cheng's profile and CBI's activities fit the profile perfectly.
Cheng himself was known as a recluse,
residing in a hypermodern villa hidden within a park-sized lot in Shanghai's Xuhui district.
"There couldn't possibly be a more supervillain-esque person matching this outrageous plot," she thought.
"He's moved back to his ancestral homeland from New York, built himself a fortress among the wealthy,
and he's famously conservative,
a control freak difficult to collaborate with if this documentation is even remotely accurate.
I need to go to Shanghai to find out more, that much is clear."

Sonia needed a way to get to Shanghai, and the path led through Clara Dumont,
her skip-level manager and the Director of Genetic Research at GAIA.
Clara oversaw all viral genome research teams, including the one that Sonia belonged to.
Sonia planned to frame her excursion as a routine field trip to collect samples,
officially to pinpoint Patient Zero for LASS-VASH-2024.
Since the findings about the virus hadn't been released yet, it was still classified as harmless but intriguing.
With Anna being Changed, Sonia wasn't sure if Anna retained any interest in escalating the situation.
Nonetheless, Sonia believed that securing approval for a standard field trip should be straightforward.
She drafted the proposal,
sent it off, and prepared to discuss it during her one-on-one meeting with Clara later that day.

As Sonia entered Clara's office, she was immediately struck by the Change in her manager.
Clara, too, was among the Changed,
something Sonia hadn't anticipated despite knowing the significant number of Changed within GAIA.
Clara's appearance was markedly different: her figure suggested the use of shapewear,
and she wore the uniform of the Changed.
In her case, an impractically narrow gray skirt,
a Victorian-inspired blouse that buttoned at the back, a hijab, and even indoors,
light blue satin gloves.

Sonia, unsure how to interpret this new version of Clara, proceeded cautiously.
The old Clara had been a figure of authority and respect;
this Changed Clara was an enigma, potentially even an adversary.
Yet, Sonia needed her approval for the mission to Shanghai.
She presented her case with calculated normalcy.

"Clara," Sonia started,
maintaining her composure, "I'd like to conduct a field trip similar to our recent work in Shenzhen,
to collect virus samples and possibly pinpoint Patient Zero."

Clara listened intently, her response was unexpectedly supportive and devoid of the hesitation Sonia had braced for.
"Of course, Sonia.
Your initiative aligns perfectly with our objectives.
Gathering more data on LASS-VASH-2024 is crucial, and I fully support your fieldwork in Shanghai."

Sonia was taken aback by Clara's quick endorsement.
It wasn't just the ease of the approval but the implicit trust Clara seemed to place in the venture.
Sonia had expected some resistance or at least scrutiny, considering the risks,
but Clara either didn't connect her own transformation with the virus or chose to ignore it.

Instead, Clara's response seemed to reflect a deep-seated belief in the benefits of the virus,
a conviction that appeared to unite those who had been Changed.
This realization made Sonia reflect on the broader implications as she left Clara's office, approval in hand.
The Changed, including Clara and previously Anna,
demonstrated an uncanny level of cooperation and positivity towards the virus and its consequences,
a phenomenon Sonia contemplated deeply as she prepared for her trip.

Sonia was more than a bit paranoid by now:
with a mind-altering virus at play, she trusted nothing and no one, not even her own judgment.
Why were the Changed so uniformly supportive of actions that sought to investigate or potentially undermine the virus?
Was it a side effect of the conditioning, a deeply ingrained trust in the virus's purpose?
Or was it something more—a strategic compliance,
fully aware that any efforts to understand the virus might inadvertently lead to its further spread?

With Clara's approval secured, Sonia shifted her focus to preparing for her journey.
She confirmed that her work visa for Shenzhen was still valid,
as were her additional documentation and permits needed as a field researcher working with biohazards.
She had currency, and her SIM card was still active in her Chinese phone.
She resolved not to use her "western" phone with any Chinese applications.
She viewed her trip to Shanghai as an opportunity to uncover the truth about the virus and its origins,
and to trace its connections to Dr. Mingyu Chen.
She wanted to better understand the role of the boutique chains that seemed to flourish in the wake of the epidemic.

Having booked her flights,
Sonia decided to visit the local Maylee Shufoo near her home.
Now that she knew it was definitively connected to the virus,
she was able to look at it differently and might be able to gain more insights.
What she discovered was that the clothing had become even more restrictive.
The new offerings at Maylee Shufoo included corsets,
which hadn't been there before, and a new item called 高抱佑护, "Gowbao Yoohoo" or "Embrace and Protect."
This variant of the well-known Victorian blouses
she was already familiar with featured a higher neck that was internally reinforced with a leather collar,
resembling a neck corset.
It was completely impractical and very restrictive,
and when completely closed would lock the wearers head in a specific position,
straight upright, gaze forward and slightly downward,
a unique combination of regal posture with submissive head position.
It buttoned at the back, and the buttons on the arms ran from the elbow to the wrist on the outside of each arm.
Opening or closing it involved dealing with dozens of minuscule buttons in unreachable locations,
impossible to work with gloves on.
Clearly, it was designed so that someone else had to help put it on or take it off.

Sonia thought deeply about the name, trying to decipher its meaning.
It suggested "high embrace" or "to hold closely," and "to protect," or "bless and protect."
Together, these conveyed a sense of care and security.
Yet, the garment itself told a different story—it was all about constraint and control.

The "high embrace" implied a suffocating closeness, leaving no room for movement or independence.
The notion of protection morphed into a form of guardianship that demanded submission.
The garment, with its tight neck and unremovable embrace, embodied the virus's goal:
to reshape people into obedient figures from the past, under the guise of care and protection.

And with each iteration, the fashions of "Beautiful Restraint" became more restrictive,
making their wearer more dependent and less autonomous.
The link between the virus and these garments showed how society was changing.
The virus and the boutiques spread their ideology of control and submission.
The world was becoming a place where old-fashioned looks hid a reality of control.

As she boarded her plane, Sonia knew: she had to act fast to find a way to fight back against the virus.
