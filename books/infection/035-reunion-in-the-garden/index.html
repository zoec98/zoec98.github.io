                                                                                                                                                  Broken by discipline, remade by reward, Mara learns to endure the iGuardian’s grip, until the Green Gala tests her obedience and her pride.

                                                                                                                                                  Two years ago, Mara was fitted with the iGuardian Pro Secure, a device that reshaped her body and mind with unrelenting pressure, posture corrections, gag-feeding, and a tormenting edging mode that broke her resistance more effectively than pain ever could. Under Philippe’s guidance, she grew into her role, proving her worth through the Community Garden Project that reshaped Geneva itself. Now, at the Green Gala, Mara must balance public grace and private vulnerability, helping Philippe spin Zendaya’s scandal into strategy, only to watch the evening collapse into a high-profile kidnapping. In the aftermath, she clings to the iGuardian for comfort, embraces pony training at Philippe’s side, and learns that even in failure, her devotion is what makes her valuable. Mara’s journey is one of discipline and desire, resistance and surrender, as she becomes not only Philippe’s ward but his partner in a Changed world built on beauty, obedience, and control.

                                                                                                                                                  Three years after the Change, Sonia had been Henry Serrurier’s Sonia for a while now.

                                                                                                                                                  Walking through Geneva’s community garden, she admired the lush surroundings. This place, once the concrete wasteland of Geneva airport, was now a thriving green space. Each plant and path showed careful attention, reflecting order and beauty.

                                                                                                                                                  She noticed a group of young women approaching from a distance. They moved in perfect synchronization, marching in an orderly double row. These were students from the École de Réforme du Nord-Ouest, a reformatory school in the northwest quarter of the city, near the former Geneva airport. The school catered to young women who struggled to adjust to their Change.

                                                                                                                                                  The students were dressed in immaculate uniforms: modest blouses with high collars, gray blazers, and ankle-length skirts that swayed slightly with each step. Their faces were concealed behind uniform porcelain masks, and their hair was hidden under neat headscarves. The overall effect was one of strict uniformity, their only identifying marker the name tags that blinked in the sun.

                                                                                                                                                  As they reached a designated area within the garden, the group came to a precise halt. With military-like efficiency, they shifted into a single row, standing at attention, their posture perfect as they awaited further instruction. Their teacher, an older woman whose uniform differed only in the color and stripes on the hem of her skirt began her inspection. She moved methodically down the line, her sharp eyes catching any deviation from the expected standard. A minor adjustment here, a slight correction there, and the line of students was once again flawless.

                                                                                                                                                  Satisfied, the teacher signaled for the next phase. The students quickly donned green gardening aprons with coordinated and fluid movements. Two women from the garden staff arrived, pushing rolling carts filled with neatly organized gardening tools. The teacher gave a series of hand signals, directing the students to work in pairs or groups of four. Tools were distributed, and the young women turned to their tasks with disciplined precision.

                                                                                                                                                  Working in the garden offered these young women a small measure of autonomy within a controlled environment. Here, amidst the greenery, they found a sense of purpose and the opportunity to contribute, while the school ensured that their behavior remained within the strict bounds of societal expectations. The garden, once a barren concrete expanse, now thrived under their care, symbolizing the balance between personal growth and the rigid structure that defined their lives.

                                                                                                                                                  Closer to her, Sonia noticed a small, graceful figure moving between the garden beds. The woman’s movements were precise, like a ballet dancer. She wore a bonnet covering her hair, and the cut of her blouse suggested a tight corset underneath, emphasizing her slim waist. She wore a crisp white Gobao Yoohoo with a high collar together with an Edwardian-cut walking skirt in a deep navy blue covered by a gardening apron in a slightly brighter shade. Her skirt did not show any rank insignia at all at the hem.

                                                                                                                                                  Every step she made showed the limitations of her clothing. Her gloved hands moved precisely as she communicated with others in the garden. The gloves were elegant yet sturdy, suitable for gardening. What was visible of her face under the bonnet was hidden behind a porcelain mask, making her hard to identify, but the elegance of her movements felt familiar to Sonia.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia watched the woman interact with groups of young uniformed women working in the garden. She guided them with a gentle but firm presence, her hand gestures clear and expressive. Her ability to command respect without speaking reminded Sonia of Mara’s strategic brilliance.

                                                                                                                                                  Mara Delacroix, known as “Mara the Merciless,” had been a corporate lawyer before the Change. She helped Sonia identify key figures behind the Change virus. Sonia lost track of Mara during the upheavals following the Change, when Sonia traveled to Shanghai for several months to get information from the people Mara had identified. When she returned later, she learned Mara had left her law practice.

                                                                                                                                                  After observing for a while, Sonia decided to approach. Standing a few steps away, Sonia took a deep breath, moved forward, and tapped the woman’s shoulder. The woman turned, and though her face was masked, Sonia sensed recognition.

                                                                                                                                                  “Mara?” Sonia signed tentatively. “It’s Sonia. I was… Sonia Becker.” She hesitated before she signed her pre-Change name. Using it felt wrong. “I am Henry Serrurier’s Sonia. It’s been a long time. How have you been?”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s gloved hands paused, then she acknowledged Sonia’s new name with a series of hand movements. “Henry Serrurier’s Sonia, I am Philippe Beaumont’s Mara,” she signed with great formality. She continued less reserved. “It is good to see you, Sonia. I am well. Thank you for asking.”

                                                                                                                                                  Relief and joy lit up Sonia’s face, her smile visible in her eyes even behind her own mask. “I thought I recognized you. You’ve done an amazing job here.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s hands moved again, fluid and precise. “Thank you. This garden is a labor of love. I’m glad you approve.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia looked around, taking in the thriving greenery and peace. “It’s beautiful. Truly. But how did you manage all this, especially after everything changed?”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s hands moved confidently. “It’s been a journey. Much has changed, including me. But I found new ways to contribute. The garden reflects our commitment to sustainability and community.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia nodded, feeling admiration for her friend. “I’d love to hear more about it. Maybe we could walk and… sign?”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara tried to nod but struggled, then gave a shallow curtsy. “I would like that very much, Sonia. I have much to share.”

                                                                                                                                                  “Following the Change,” Mara signed, “I found that I couldn’t continue my legal career. And I needed to find a guardian.” She hesitated, and Sonia understood her situation.

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s fierce independence had been subdued by necessity. Sonia understood the pressure to conform and the constant scrutiny that came with it—she had lived through it herself.

                                                                                                                                                  “I found Philippe, and he took me as his ward. He suggested I find a project that aligns with societal values. The community garden came from that idea.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia nodded, taking in Mara’s story. “What you’ve accomplished here is amazing, Mara.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s signs became more formal. “I just helped make it happen. The community did the work.”

                                                                                                                                                  As they walked, a uniformed young woman approached, looking uncertain.

                                                                                                                                                  “Madam Mara,” she signed, “I need help with the composting area. The new bins arrived, but I’m not sure how to set them up.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara responded right away. “Of course. Get Camille. She knows the setup and will teach you. I’ll check on it later.” The young woman nodded and hurried off.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia noticed the respect in the young woman’s eyes. Mara wasn’t just a facilitator; she was a leader. They continued walking, and Mara resumed her story.

                                                                                                                                                  “The garden is also a place for learning and rehabilitation,” she signed. “We run programs for young women from difficult backgrounds, helping them adjust to the Change. They learn about sustainability and find purpose in their work here.”

                                                                                                                                                  They paused by a group of women tending to a herb garden. Mara gently corrected one woman’s technique with a few precise hand movements. The woman’s gratitude was clear.

                                                                                                                                                  Further along, they came across two men in tailored suits, clearly important figures. Sonia immediately stepped back, curtsied, and waited, as was expected of her in such situations.

                                                                                                                                                  “Madam Mara, if you have a moment?” Councillor Laurent spoke with authority. Mara curtsied and signed her greeting.

                                                                                                                                                  “We’re discussing the garden expansion. There are concerns about funding and logistics.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s eyes brightened behind her mask as she signed her response. “Councillor Laurent, I appreciate your concerns. I might be able to offer a solution if it suits your planning. Minister Jacques Laborde provides a National Sustainability Fund.” Mara produced a slim binder from the depths of her skirts. “If you kindly check the paperwork, you will find a prepared proposal for your review. With your approval, it could be sent to the ministry.”

                                                                                                                                                  The other man, Councillor Dubois, looked interested. “A National Sustainability Fund? That could solve our problems.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara explained further. “Yes, Sir. The proposal shows how the garden meets the fund’s goals, making it eligible for 75 percent funding. Securing this grant would highlight your commitment to environmental initiatives.”

                                                                                                                                                  Laurent took the proposal from Mara, glancing through it. “This could indeed show our dedication to sustainability. We’ll review it thoroughly.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara curtsied deeply. “Thank you, Sirs. I’m here to support the community in any way I can.”

                                                                                                                                                  As the men walked away, Sonia admired how Mara handled the situation. “You manage them so well, Mara. It’s like you’re orchestrating everything behind the scenes.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara responded with the same humble modesty. “I just offer guidance. The true credit belongs to the community.”

                                                                                                                                                  As they walked, Sonia couldn’t shake her awe. Mara had adapted to Changed society and found a way to thrive. Her skill in influencing and guiding without overstepping was remarkable. Yet, it was clear that the Mara she spoke to was a persona for dealing with society.

                                                                                                                                                  Still, Sonia was impressed. “Mara, you’re amazing,” she signed with admiration. “I’m so glad I found you again.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara’s hands moved smoothly. “And I’m glad you are here, Sonia. Together…” Her hands froze mid-sign, and she made a muffled noise behind her mask. Then she stopped and stood very still, a subtle tremor in her posture. “I have to go,” Mara signed quickly, her hands trembling slightly.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia’s eyes widened with concern. “Mara, is everything okay?”

                                                                                                                                                  Mara signed her response, movements restrained. “Yes, Sonia. I have brought this upon myself. It’s the iGuardian switching to discipline mode because I’m late.” She paused, then added, “Please don’t worry. I’ll be fine.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia watched as Mara glided away with small steps, her hard heels making rapid clicking noises on the paving slabs. Despite her discomfort, Mara maintained her poise, showing years of training and adaptation.

                                                                                                                                                  Understanding dawned on Sonia. The iGuardian, the latest product out of Cupertino, was somewhat infamous among wards. Sonia had been in chastity belts for many years, but never in this particular device. However, she had heard enough stories to grasp its reputation. Designed as an electronic chastity and discipline product, it was sleek and comfortable for long-term wear, offering exquisite rewards for obedient behavior. However, it was equally renowned for its corrections when in discipline mode.

                                                                                                                                                  As Mara moved away, Sonia marveled at Mara’s ability to maintain composure and continue her influential work despite the restraints and potential for correction.

                                                                                                                                                  A young woman approached Sonia and curtsied. “Excuse me, Madam,” she signed. “Can I help you with anything? I saw you with Madam Mara.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia smiled and signed back. “Thank you, but I’m fine. Madam Mara had to attend to something urgent. I was just admiring the work here.”

                                                                                                                                                  The young woman nodded, pride visible in her eyes. “We’re all very dedicated to the garden. Madam Mara’s guidance is invaluable.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia’s heart warmed. “It’s truly impressive. The dedication and effort are remarkable.”

                                                                                                                                                  The young woman beamed and excused herself. Sonia took a deep, corset-restricted breath, reflecting on their conversation. Despite the strict adherence to Changed principles, there was a vibrant energy in the garden. Mara had cultivated not just plants, but a community committed to a greater purpose, and Sonia resolved to become part of it.

                                                                                                                                                  Meanwhile, Mara hurried through the garden paths. The iGuardian was relentless, driving her onward with painful impulses into her rectum and guiding her with sharp signals in her breast electrodes. The stiff collar of her neck corset, integrated with her mask and gag, prevented her from turning her head at all, forcing her to rely on limited vision.

                                                                                                                                                  Each step was a struggle against the escalating corrections, bordering on agony. She had only herself to blame; she knew better than to be late to appointments with Philippe. Overwhelmed by her reunion with Sonia, she lost track of time. Her mask restricted her breathing, and the gag filled her mouth uncomfortably. The electrodes continued their punishing rhythm, a constant reminder of her need for punctuality.
