# Maid service

By the time they finished unloading the equipment at Henry's villa, Sonia should have gone home.

That was the sensible version of the evening. Thank him, leave, take a shower, sit alone with the afterimage of the hood and the chains and try to decide what any of it meant before seeing him again.

Instead she heard herself say, "If you are not in a hurry, Sir, would you let me cook for you?"

Henry looked at her for one measured second.

"At your place?"

"Yes."

"All right."

The drive across Geneva felt stranger than the mountain road had. Less dramatic, more intimate. They were no longer heading toward an agreed experiment. They were carrying its consequences back into ordinary streets.

Once inside her apartment, Sonia asked him to wait in the sitting room and went to change. She opened the wardrobe, looked at her work clothes, looked at the maid dress folded behind them, and understood before she touched it that this was not costume. It was hunger.

She put it on anyway.

The black dress closed around her with the familiar firmness of a role that knew exactly what it wanted from her. White apron. Collar. Gloves. Work heels, if such a thing existed. By the time she tied the last bow, her breathing had steadied. The turmoil from the drive had narrowed into something usable.

When she entered the sitting room, Henry was standing near the bookshelf, looking at a framed photograph on the mantel.

He turned.

The silence that followed was not surprise. It was recognition.

Sonia lowered her gaze and curtsied.

"If it pleases you, Sir, your maid would like to serve supper."

"Would she."

"Yes, Sir."

He set the frame down carefully.

"Then serve."

That one word settled her more effectively than any reassurance could have done.

She cooked something simple: pasta, butter, herbs, a little lemon, anchovies worked down into the pan until they became depth rather than fish. Henry sat where he could watch her. Sonia was aware of him every second, not in a nervous way, but in the way a compass is aware of north. She moved more quietly than usual, more efficiently too, because the role simplified the work. Chop, stir, plate, wipe, carry. No spare motion. No spare self.

When she set the plate before him, she asked, "May this humble maid stand by, Sir?"

"Yes."

She remained beside the table with her hands folded, head slightly bowed, and felt the old Shanghai discipline return with a force that should have alarmed her more than it did. The difference was that this time she was not doing it for cover. She was doing it because being useful to Henry made the room feel arranged correctly.

He ate. She watched without staring.

"Take your place," he said at one point.

Sonia looked up before she could stop herself.

"Sir?"

"Not at the table. There."

He touched the rug beside his chair with two fingers.

She went down at once, knees together, back straight. The position was familiar enough to make her chest tighten. Henry resumed eating as if a woman at his knee were the most ordinary extension of supper in the world.

The peace of it hit her so suddenly she almost resented it.

"You enjoy this," he said.

It was not a question.

Sonia kept her eyes lowered.

"Yes, Sir."

"How much?"

That answer took longer.

"Enough that it frightens me a little."

He finished the mouthful he was on before answering.

"Fear means you are paying attention."

The line should have irritated her. Instead it relieved her, because it let the fear remain inside the room without forcing it to become a crisis.

When he finished eating, she cleared the table, washed the dishes, wiped down the counter, and restored the kitchen with the satisfaction of a person allowed to do the work she had been craving all evening. Henry did not praise every motion, he let the role stand. He expected it to continue.

When the apartment was in order, he called her back into the sitting room.

"Come here."

She crossed to him and curtsied again.

"What else does my maid want tonight?"

The question went through her like a key turning.

She could have answered politely. Could have said whatever pleased you, Sir, and left it at that. Instead the truth came out before she could polish it.

"To be useful. To be looked after. To stay like this a little longer."

Henry sat back, studying her.

"Then kneel between my knees."

She obeyed.

Once there, close enough to feel the heat of him, Sonia understood that the maid role had carried her much further than an outfit should have been able to. She was not playacting service. She was inside it. Her body had already organized itself around the logic of command, usefulness, attention, approval.

Henry rested one hand lightly at the side of her neck.

"Look at me."

She did.

"You can still stop."

That offer mattered. She knew it mattered. It also made stopping feel less possible rather than more.

"I do not want to stop, Sir."

"No?"

"No, Sir."

He let his thumb brush once along the line beneath her jaw, not soft enough to be casual and not hard enough to count as force, just enough to keep her exactly where he wanted her while he watched her answer him.

"Then serve dessert."

She breathed out, slow and shaky.

"Yes, Sir."

The act itself was less important than what it did to her. Sonia had expected explicitness to break the spell, to return her to herself in some sharp embarrassed way. Instead it carried the service deeper. She unfastened him, took him into her mouth, and found that the same clean narrowing from the shoot could happen here too: task, body, praise anticipated. His hand in her hair. His voice above her. Her own pleasure threaded through obedience so tightly she could not separate them without lying.

At first she tried to tease him, hold him just at the edge, stay in control of the pace because part of her still wanted proof that she had not vanished into the role entirely.

Henry felt it at once.

His fingers tightened just enough at the back of her head.

"No games now," he said. "Finish properly."

The command landed as heat low in her body.

She obeyed with a devotion that shocked her.

When he came, when she stayed where he held her and took what he gave, the relief was not merely sexual. It was structural. She had been told what to do and had done it well. Her whole body lit around that fact.

She sat back on her heels afterward, breathing carefully, hands on her thighs, eyes down.

"Thank you for using your maid, Sir."

Henry's hand came under her chin and lifted her face.

"Good girl."

There it was.

Anna had tried to explain it once. Sonia had understood intellectually and dismissed half of it as viral softness.

This was not intellectual. The praise struck cleanly and far too deep, spreading through her in a hard bright rush that left her damp, weak, and abruptly ashamed of how much she wanted more of it.

Her forehead came to rest against his knee without decision.

He stroked her hair once.

"Stay."

She stayed.

They did not rush into another explicit scene after that. That mattered. Henry let the praise and the service and the collapse stand on their own. Later he made tea. She served it. He asked for water. She brought it. The apartment slipped into a quieter version of the same order until it was too late to pretend the evening still belonged to ordinary categories.

When he rose to leave, Sonia surprised both of them by saying, "Please don't."

Henry turned back.

She swallowed.

"Sir."

He walked to her slowly.

"You want me to stay?"

"Yes, Sir."

The answer was humiliating. It was also the most honest thing she had said all day.

He looked at the maid dress, the careful room, the strain she was putting into standing upright.

"All right," he said. "I will stay. But you are not sleeping alone in that state."

She almost said thank you and stopped herself only because the gratitude was too large for the sentence.

Henry made her a nest of cushions and blankets at the foot of her bed. He would have let her undress, she thought, if she had asked. She did not ask. Remaining corseted, remaining in the maid dress, remaining arranged under his supervision felt less like deprivation than like continuity.

"Lie down," he said.

She did.

"Blanket."

She drew it over herself.

"Wake me at seven."

"Yes, Sir."

He slept in her bed while she slept at its foot and somehow that did not feel insulting. It felt exactly as if things were as they should be. She drifted in and out with the deep tiredness that follows a drop too complete to understand yet. The corset pressed at every breath. The memory of his praise stayed warm in her body long after the physical arousal had ebbed.

At dawn she woke before the alarm, washed, refreshed herself, and put the room back into order. Then she woke him with her mouth because the thought had come to her while she was laying out breakfast and once it arrived it had felt less like an invention than like the obvious correct way to begin his day.

He woke with a hand in her hair and a low sound she felt more than heard.

Later, breakfast was on the table, the cushions were stacked away, and Sonia knelt before him on the rug while sunlight moved gradually across her floorboards.

"Talk to me," he said.

The first person came back more slowly than she expected.

"About what, Sir?"

"About yesterday. About this."

She looked at her own hands in her lap.

"I thought the maid role would feel like an indulgence." She paused. "It does not. It feels like a door. When I go through it, I stop wasting energy on the parts of myself that want to argue for the sake of arguing."

Henry said nothing. That helped.

"In Shanghai I found it comforting and hated that I found it comforting. Yesterday with you..." She stopped and started again. "Yesterday with you it went further than comfort. I wanted the role. I wanted the commands. I wanted to make myself useful and stay there."

"And the praise?"

Her face heated.

"Yes, Sir."

"What about it?"

She forced herself to answer directly.

"It felt like something opening." Her voice dropped. "Something I have been standing in front of for a long time."

Henry leaned forward slightly.

"Do you think this is a game?"

"No, Sir."

"Do you think it is the virus?"

That was the old emergency exit. She heard it and refused it.

"No." Sonia lifted her eyes to him for the first time in several sentences. "I think the virus made the world easier to read. I think Shanghai showed me something. I think yesterday proved I want it. But wanting it is mine."

He held her gaze for a moment, then nodded.

"Very well."

The words warmed her again.

"Because if you continue with me, Sonia, I am not interested in a decorative submission you wear on weekends and analyze to death afterward. I want something lived."

She drew in a careful breath.

"I know."

"Do you."

She thought of the maid dress on her body, the rug under her knees, the peace she had felt with his plate in her hands, the humiliating purity of good girl, the calm of sleeping at his feet because he had told her to.

"Yes, Sir," she said. "I do."

By the time he left that morning, Sonia was still not ready to name what had changed. She only knew that the maid role no longer felt like cover, or experiment, or even kink in any limited sense. It felt like an access point into a life where belonging to someone in the right way might make her more free, not less.
