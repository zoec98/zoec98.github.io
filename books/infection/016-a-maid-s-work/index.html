                                                                                                                                                  A Maid’s work#

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia applied for the position, presenting herself as the perfect candidate. The interview process went quickly, either due to her qualifications or the efficiency of the Cheng household. Once she got the job, she felt triumph, but also apprehension. It was a unique chance to advance her mission, but it meant diving deeper into a lifestyle she had resisted.

                                                                                                                                                  Moving into the small, plain maid’s room in the attic of Dr. Cheng’s villa, Sonia faced the reality of her new life. The room, bare and impersonal, starkly reminded her of her role. Her few functional belongings underscored her commitment to her mission. As she arranged her things, she felt a deep sense of isolation. She was part of the household now, yet remained an outsider, a spy in a world both foreign and crucial to her quest.

                                                                                                                                                  Living in Dr. Cheng’s mansion, Sonia found herself in a stricter, more anonymous setting than her training. Each morning, as she put on her uniform, the reflection in the mirror sparked a sense of loss. The corset, with its shoulder braces, forced her body into a rigid form, while the narrow underskirt severely limited her movements. The ankle-length gray maid’s dress, with a high collar and gloves, erased any trace of personal identity, allowing her to blend into the background.

                                                                                                                                                  Roll call became a daily ritual of conformity. Lined up with the other maids, Sonia stood for inspection, her makeup flawlessly applied to meet the household’s high standards. This routine highlighted the erosion of her individuality, a necessary sacrifice for her ultimate goal.

                                                                                                                                                  Inside Dr. Cheng’s mansion, Sonia moved like a shadow, unnoticed among the other uniformed staff. She had no name or identity; she was just a maid, a role performing services. Silent, but useful and pleasant to the eye.

                                                                                                                                                  Discipline was strict. In public spaces, Sonia had to be silent, impeccably dressed, and either working or waiting for orders, standing discreetly by a door or table. As Mei Lin had trained her, she couldn’t allow herself to relax. Her mind stayed focused on her superiors, ready to serve at any moment. The strictness of her uniform helped, constantly reminding her whenever she relaxed her shoulders or slouched—behaviors that would lead to discipline if noticed.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia struggled not to lose herself in the monotonous routines of service. It was easy for her mind to drift, focusing only on immediate tasks and the sensory input from her uniform, adapting to its constraints. She concentrated on the person she served; their identity mattered no more than her own. She existed only to serve.

                                                                                                                                                  The housekeeper, Mrs. Huang, managed the staff with an iron fist. Her sharp commands sliced through the air. She was a stern woman, always watching closely, quick to point out any imperfection. “You missed a spot, Su ni ya. Do it again, and this time, pretend your job depends on it, because it does!” her voice would echo down the hallways, making the newer maids jump.

                                                                                                                                                  The butler, Mr. Chen, while equally strict, maintained an air of polite formality. “Miss Sonia, if you would kindly ensure that the silver is polished to a mirror finish. We aim for excellence in all things here,” he would say, his tone firm yet wrapped in a veneer of civility, underscoring his demand for perfection.

                                                                                                                                                  Within the vast quiet of Dr. Cheng’s mansion, Sonia was nearly invisible, her voice reduced to a mantra of submission. “Yes, sir,” “Of course, sir,” and “Is there anything else this humble maid can do for you, sir?” These phrases echoed only when directly addressed by her employer or his guests. She remained silent unless spoken to. When referring to herself, she used her role, never the first person—she was not “I” nor “Sonia”; she was “this humble maid.”

                                                                                                                                                  Among the staff, communication often unfolded through a silent ballet of gestures and movements, a language Sonia found surprisingly familiar. They used 手舞言, Show-woo-yen, or “Showyou,” the hand-dancing language. It was a tool she wielded with unexpected grace, another unforeseen gift from the virus that had altered her in so many ways.

                                                                                                                                                  Work took her to all parts of the large building, and Sonia quickly memorized the sprawling layout of Dr. Cheng’s mansion, committing the twists and turns of its opulent corridors to memory. She noted the locations of key areas, like Dr. Cheng’s personal office and the laboratory spaces. Both were off-limits to staff unless for specific tasks like special cleaning or supply runs, and only under direct orders.

                                                                                                                                                  One crisp morning, Sonia received a summons from Mrs. Huang. “You are to retrieve a briefcase from Dr. Cheng’s quarters and deliver it to the conference room. Do not dally,” Mrs. Huang instructed, her tone leaving no room for argument.

                                                                                                                                                  With the rapid clicking of her heels through the narrow servant corridors, Sonia navigated to Dr. Cheng’s private quarters, entering through a hidden servant’s door. She found the briefcase on a small desk in his bedroom, heavy with papers and a tablet. Grabbing it, she hurried back to the conference room, her steps tiny and quick, panting against the tightness of her corset. She paused behind the servant’s entrance to catch her breath, smoothing her skirts to fix her appearance, then entered the conference room. Dr. Cheng was deep in discussion with his team of genetic engineers. Placing the briefcase beside him, Sonia retreated to the wall, blending into the ornate decor while keeping her senses alert.

                                                                                                                                                  Dr. Cheng’s voice filled the room, his passion for his vision evident. “We are on the brink of a new era,” he declared, discussing how the Change was meant to foster a collectivist society to address global issues like climate change and inequality. His words, filled with idealism, struck Sonia as ironic coming from a man of such wealth.

                                                                                                                                                  As the conversation turned to technical aspects and timelines, Sonia remained perfectly still, her training keeping her attentive yet unobtrusive. The stiffness of her attire, with the corset compressing her torso and the blouse collar grazing her chin, made even small movements feel laborious. Yet, she performed her role flawlessly, moving only to serve drinks or adjust settings at the slightest indication—a nod, a gesture.

                                                                                                                                                  Dr. Cheng outlined the timeline for the Changed to influence laws upholding societal norms. “When do we anticipate reaching the infiltration threshold needed to begin legal transformations?” he asked pointedly. The engineers scrambled through their data, providing estimates and scenarios that painted a future reshaped by their work.

                                                                                                                                                  Throughout the meeting, Sonia’s emotions swung between awe at the scope of Dr. Cheng’s ambitions and a growing resolve to stop them. The realization that she was privy to a plan seeking to alter human society fundamentally was chilling. Every discussion about timelines and societal changes, every casual reference to the deep and permanent impact of the Change, reinforced her determination to expose the truth.

                                                                                                                                                  As the meeting adjourned and Dr. Cheng and his team left the room, Sonia lingered under the pretense of cleaning up, her mind racing with the information she had gathered. Exiting the conference room, Sonia’s steps were careful, the whisper of her skirts and the click of her heels echoing softly in the empty hallway. She was more than just a maid in a billionaire’s mansion; she was a spy at the heart of an operation that could decide the fate of humanity. Reminding herself of her mission, Sonia kept her eyes and ears open for any snippet of conversation or overlooked document. Anything that might lead her closer to understanding the true nature of the virus that had changed the world and forced her to become… this.

                                                                                                                                                  One afternoon, Mr. Chen called Sonia over. “Miss Sonia, Dr. Cheng wants his office floors waxed tonight. You’ll handle it yourself,” he said, handing her the keys with a nod to show the task’s importance. Dr. Cheng was not present that day; he had gone to a new fashion show by Mirende Ginciao, one of the few things that could draw him away from his sanctuary. It seemed to be a big event, introducing a major new fashion item.

                                                                                                                                                  Seeing her chance, Sonia used her role as a maid to slip into the lab under the guise of her duties. Inside, she moved quickly, her gloved hands searching through the lab despite the restrictions of her outfit. She opened drawers and flipped through files, balancing speed with the need to stay unnoticed. Her pulse quickened when she found USB sticks hidden among the lab’s clutter.

                                                                                                                                                  She transferred the files to her phone with speed and precision. Each copied file brought her closer to understanding, another piece of the puzzle that had led her halfway around the world. The fabric of her dress brushed against her skin as she moved, a constant reminder of the world she was fighting against.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia’s heart raced as she heard Dr. Cheng return, her actions in the lab just moments away from discovery. With practiced submissiveness, she curtsied, the stiffness of her uniform sharply reminding her of her role in this elaborate act. Her exit from the lab was as quiet as a whisper, leaving Dr. Cheng unaware of the theft that had just occurred right under his nose. Once back in her small, spartan room, Sonia felt the weight of her corset and the high collar of her uniform. Her phone, filled with stolen secrets, lay in her hands. The files she had transferred spoke of a virus meant not just to infect bodies but to reshape society with forced ideals. Sonia’s mind raced as she uploaded everything to the cloud, carefully making another copy of the encrypted data on her phone.

                                                                                                                                                  Approaching Mrs. Huang cautiously, Sonia spoke, her voice trembling slightly. “Mrs. Huang, Madam, if I may,” Sonia began, eyes lowered. “My mother is gravely ill, and I humbly ask for some time off to care for her.”

                                                                                                                                                  Mrs. Huang, ever the strict overseer, arched an eyebrow, her expression stern. “Su ni ya,” she said sharply, “you know the rules. Your duties here come first. Personal issues can’t interfere.”

                                                                                                                                                  Just as Mrs. Huang was about to refuse, Mr. Chen, the butler, entered the room. He had overheard the conversation, and his demeanor was one of controlled concern, reflecting the Changed values on family importance. “Mrs. Huang, may I have a word?” he asked smoothly, his voice calm yet authoritative.

                                                                                                                                                  “Of course, Mr. Chen,” Mrs. Huang replied, her tone shifting to one of deference to her immediate superior in the household hierarchy.

                                                                                                                                                  Mr. Chen turned to Sonia, serious. “Miss Sonia,” he began, “while your primary duty is here, we must remember the core values Dr. Cheng instills in us, including family. You may take time to attend to your mother. However, remember, you are an extension of this household’s values, even outside these walls.”

                                                                                                                                                  “Thank you, sir,” Sonia responded, curtsying. “I appreciate your understanding and promise to uphold our standards and represent this household with respect and dedication.” The lies slipped smoothly from her lips.

                                                                                                                                                  Mr. Chen nodded, a subtle smile softening his stern features. “See that you do, Sonia. We consider you part of this family too. I expect you to carry yourself as a member of this household should, even in personal trials. Go, attend to your mother, and return with dignity.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia curtsied deeply, her heart racing from the lie. “Thank you sincerely, sir. I won’t disappoint you or this esteemed household.”

                                                                                                                                                  As Sonia left the room, her mind raced through her escape plan, knowing every second Mrs. Huang and Mr. Chen remained unaware of her permanent departure was precious time to reach safety.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia booked a flight home with urgency. She had no intention to formally quit or return, relying on the protection of her faked credentials and the distance she intended to put between herself and Dr. Cheng’s household. The data on her phone, now encrypted and stored in the cloud, was a key that could unlock the truth about the virus and its creators.

                                                                                                                                                  Landside at the airport, Sonia’s eyes caught a display that chilled her to the core. It was a showcase from Mirende Ginciao, the high-fashion boutique known for its restrictive designs. These accessories were the reason Dr. Cheng had been away. The display featured a new line of masks called Jinshuwan—simple yet sinister half-masks covering the mouth and nose, stretching from ear to ear. They were marketed as affordable and practical, made from lightweight materials, less intimidating but no less controlling.

                                                                                                                                                  Next to them were the luxurious Jurenmuse masks. These were full-face masks, beautifully crafted from porcelain and fine materials, polished to a reflective shine. They left only the eyes visible, turning wearers into living statues, objects of silent beauty. Each mask came with a bit-gag, a built-in silicone silencer inserted into the mouth. Unlike traditional masks, these stayed in place by the gag, ensuring silence was both compulsory and conspicuous.

                                                                                                                                                  The significance of these masks hit Sonia hard—the silence, the forced submission, the erasure of identity. It was a chilling confirmation of the society’s push for total control, especially over women. The use of Showyou, the hand-dancing language she shared with the maids, suddenly made frightening sense. What seemed a discreet way to communicate was part of a larger plan to silence women’s voices altogether.

                                                                                                                                                  Standing there, staring at the display, Sonia felt anger mixed with fear. She muttered to herself, a blend of horror and resolve. “This… this is their plan. Complete silence, total submission. They’ve moved from shaping bodies to sealing lips, erasing personal expression. And we… we’ve been part of this without knowing.”

                                                                                                                                                  The bit gags were a brutal twist. “They’re not just taking away our ability to speak; they’re physically gagging us, making us complicit in our own silencing. These gags… they’re a physical manifestation of the virus’s metaphorical muzzle.”

                                                                                                                                                  With renewed urgency, Sonia moved through security and headed to the gate. She held data that could expose all this, that could maybe stop it. Clutching her phone, her resolve hardened. “I have to organize a resistance. No matter what it takes, this… this cannot be the future.”
