                                                                                                                                                  Good Girl#

                                                                                                                                                  Anna walked slowly back to her apartment, her steps limited by her Yoyah Sheengzoh. She walked, deeply in thought about Sonia’s unreasonable and unseemly behavior and her sloppy clothing, considering staging an intervention, because Sonia clearly needed help. Returning to her apartment, Anna climbed the stairs, the tight underskirt constraining her movements and forcing her knees to stay together, making the ascent more complicated. She had to stand sideways and hold on to the rail with gloved hands, lifting her left foot to the next stair and then pulling her right foot to join it, repeating this process step by step. This method was something she remembered her mother teaching her to navigate stairs in tight skirts. It felt normal and proper.

                                                                                                                                                  Reaching her floor, she encountered her neighbor who still seemed slightly ill, or perhaps just recovering. Next to him lay several bags with groceries and other supplies. It appeared he had suffered the same infection she had and was restocking after being unwell. She felt the urge to help, but she had to do so within the new behavioral rules that now dictated her actions. They had been living next to each other for several months, exchanging only brief greetings in the hallway, without a proper formal introduction. Anna knew this was something that needed to be addressed. As a woman, initiating an introduction was not ideal, but under the circumstances, it seemed necessary.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna curtsied and kept her gaze lowered, just as her mother had taught her. “Excuse me, Sir,” she began in a very polite voice. “I am Marc Lefèvre’s Anna, from just down the hall.” As was proper for a woman, she introduced herself in relation to the man to whom she owed her obedience, in her case, her father, since she was unmarried.

                                                                                                                                                  “I hope my forwardness does not distress you, and I beg your pardon for noting your discomfort. Should you find it agreeable, I offer my humble assistance in whatever manner you might require.” She knew she had to be very polite toward men. Then she maintained her waiting pose, her hands folded in front of her, her gaze downturned.

                                                                                                                                                  Her neighbor looked at her with surprise, due to her boldness, but also with gratitude. “Thank you, Miss Anna. I’m Julien Moreau,” he replied, his voice still tinged with exhaustion from the sickness. “Indeed, I find myself quite exhausted and could use some assistance. Would you be so kind as to help me to get my supplies inside?”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna curtsied again, the gesture almost automatic. She had been practicing this polite behavior since her early childhood, and she had been reminded by other women whenever she forgot. Her new mindset compelled her to act on orders given by a man. Following her conditioning, she stepped inside Julien’s apartment with him, picking up the groceries.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien’s apartment was the mirror image of hers, a large open plan living room with an open kitchen, a dining area, a small office setup and a large living area. Only in his case, he had a large office area where she had her living area, with several computers and a large setup with multiple screens. Off the open plan living space was a door that led to a room that was probably a bedroom with an en suite bathroom, and another door that, if the setup truly mirrored hers, the guest bathroom.

                                                                                                                                                  Stepping into Julien Moreau’s home, Anna noted the disarray caused by his illness. What was once a tidy space now reflected the chaos of his struggle with the sickness. The mess disturbed her, not merely because it was unsightly, but because it interfered with the conditioned rewards she had come to crave. She recognized that cleaning could restore the approval she now craved. “Sir, may I offer my assistance to tidy up?” she asked, her voice embodying the humility her role demanded. This felt natural to her, a way to regain the comfort of approval from her internal voice, even if she didn’t fully understand why.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien, still weakened from the infection, was surprised by her offer. His response, influenced by his own conditioning, carried a note of gratitude. “That would be most appreciated, Miss Anna,” he replied, his tone reflecting a change in their usual interaction.

                                                                                                                                                  As Anna moved through Julien’s space, her actions were meticulous and restrained by the garments defining her appearance. She maintained a composed posture, her movements limited yet careful. In the act of tidying, she rediscovered her sense of purpose, righting her after her unseemly confrontation with Sonia over breakfast. Her conditioning rewarded her once more, reinforcing her place and peace in this new order.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien observed her, his expectations now shaped by traditional views. With every task she completed, he offered praise. “Excellent work, Anna. You truly excel at this.” These accolades stirred a mix of emotions within her; they were unsettling yet deeply fulfilling. She felt deep pleasure from his acknowledgement, a sensation that ran through her with every word of approval. It was… actually making her wet.

                                                                                                                                                  The phrase “Obedience is pleasure,” often mentioned by her mother, Elena, echoed in her thoughts, now resonating more clearly than ever. Each time Julien praised her, it reinforced the virus’s programming inside her, cementing her feelings of contentment, harmony, and delight in her compliance.

                                                                                                                                                  Each of Julien’s acknowledgments and compliments further solidified her identity as shaped by the virus. The realization that her satisfaction was tied to compliance, to being seen as useful and obedient, was intoxicating.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien’s traditional expectations and his presence demonstrated how much she had changed. As she cleaned, she kept her eyes downcast, showing respect. Deep within her, the last remnants of her former self were overwhelmed by the pleasure that obedience brought. She no longer remembered the times when she had choices, when she could decide whether to serve or not. Remembering these things was no longer necessary; it was only important to serve and find happiness in it.

                                                                                                                                                  In Julien’s now tidy apartment, a heavy quiet settled between them. Julien seemed lost, surrounded by order yet internally confused. Sensing his discomfort, Anna spoke softly, attempting to show empathy and understanding.

                                                                                                                                                  “Sir,” Anna said, standing straight and humble, “to serve and to fulfill the respective expectations laid upon us brings a form of peace, an acceptance of the roles we now inhabit.” She spoke in a soft and womanly tone, suggesting rather than instructing. Then she waited, hands folded and with a downturned gaze.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien looked at her, his internal struggle apparent. “Peace? In… obeying?” he asked, his voice curious but uncertain.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna nodded, maintaining her respectful demeanor. “Yes, Sir. As a woman, obeying and following the rules has become my pleasure. It was hard at first, but there’s calmness in accepting our new path. Perhaps you can find peace in accepting your changes too. They are different from mine, but I hope they bring you peace as they have for me.”

                                                                                                                                                  Her words resonated sincerely, prompting Julien to reflect. He sat quietly for a moment before responding, “Anna, you seem… calm. If this new way can bring peace, then maybe I should try to accept my changes too.”

                                                                                                                                                  Encouraged by Julien’s response, Anna smiled slightly. “Sir, if you want to talk more or need help finding peace, I’m at your disposal.”

                                                                                                                                                  Julien stood up, noticeably more composed than before. It was a sign he was beginning to accept things. “Thank you, Miss Anna,” he said, his tone now more assertive. “I expect to see you again tomorrow before work. Your presence… it helps.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna curtsied gracefully and automatically. “Of course, I will be here, Sir,” she replied, her voice affirming her acceptance of her role. Julien nodded and said, “Good girl.” This praise triggered a rush of emotion in Anna, a feeling of validation and pleasure in her obedience and service that was intensely gratifying. It almost had a sexual intensity. It certainly made her wet again. She longed to hear this more often from Julien; in fact, in her mind, whenever she felt like this, she imagined herself as “Julien Moreau’s Anna.”

                                                                                                                                                  Back in her apartment, Anna ordered the few things that were out of place in her own apartment. Each action reinforced her commitment to her new identity. As she worked, she reflected on the day and how much she had changed.

                                                                                                                                                  The virus had reshaped her, instilling new ideas and behaviors. Anna knew her new personality wasn’t entirely her own, but she embraced it. The virus changed her, and that was good. She also always had been like this, and while this was contradictory, it was okay. The conditioning and expectations felt right now, and acknowledging them as constructs only made her programming more resilient. She felt proud of her neat and orderly apartment; it was a testament to her hard work and new purpose. The conditioning, the expectations, the pleasure derived from compliance—it all harmonized perfectly. She no longer resisted any of it.

                                                                                                                                                  “Good girl,” Anna whispered to herself, recalling Julien’s words. It made her feel calm and at peace.
