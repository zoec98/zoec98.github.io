                                                                                                                                                  Chapter 49: Sonia remembers

                                                                                                                                                  • Sonia remembers

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia remembers#

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia smiled softly as memories of her early days with Henry came back to her. She recalled the corrections, the intense training, and how her behavior gradually changed. Henry had guided her carefully, with each correction aimed at helping her improve. As she embraced submission, she realized it didn’t take away from who she was. This was striking, considering her progressive upbringing, where female submission was seen as betraying her “sisters.”

                                                                                                                                                  Growing up, Sonia had been taught to value autonomy and equality above everything else. Submission was viewed as weakness, something her family could never accept. But her time in Shanghai showed her a different side of herself. Working and training as a maid allowed her to let go of her ego, quiet her sense of self, and find joy in serving others. At first, she felt guilty and confused, worried that she was betraying the values she’d grown up with.

                                                                                                                                                  But with Henry, Sonia learned that submission didn’t make her less of a person. Instead, it was freeing to give herself fully to someone she trusted, someone who saw her submission as a gift, not a flaw. Henry’s guidance helped her understand that choosing to submit wasn’t a denial of her worth but a way of affirming it. Her submission didn’t diminish her—it enhanced who she was.

                                                                                                                                                  At GAIA, Sonia maintained a professional image. But at home, she became Henry’s maid, with no personal identity. She stopped referring to herself in the first person, even in her thoughts. She was just “this humble maid.” Her entire focus shifted to Henry—his needs, his desires. She found peace in shedding her individual identity and becoming fully absorbed in her role.

                                                                                                                                                  Balancing her public and private lives had been hard at first. After long days at GAIA, she would come home, change into her maid uniform, and try to leave “Sonia” behind. But with Henry’s firm guidance, she learned to make the transition smoother. “Your value is in your service. Be precise and disciplined; be the perfect maid. That’s what defines you,” he told her. His words resonated deeply, bringing her back to her time in Shanghai. Under his direction, she fell into those familiar habits with ease.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia repeated Henry’s words like a mantra. Her service was her gift to him, and she gave herself completely. In doing so, she found her true self—a self hidden beneath her upbringing and education.

                                                                                                                                                  Henry accepted her gift gracefully, correcting her when needed and offering rewards sparingly. He used her fully but always stopped just short, the unyielding metal of her chastity belt remained a constant reminder of her unfulfilled desire, a silent echo of her commitment. Sometimes she regretted agreeing to wear it, but she knew she was bound by her promise, both by the belt and by her unfulfilled desire. It kept her from touching herself, and kept Henry out. Though the longing remained, she eventually understood that her own desires were unimportant. She was a maid, meant to serve, to fulfill the desires of her betters, and to bring them joy. It took months for her to fully grasp this lesson.

                                                                                                                                                  At night, Sonia repeated her mantras, just as she had in Shanghai—a thousand repetitions to make one truth. Her thoughts focused on Henry—his touch, his voice, his commands. She remembered the look in his eyes when she presented herself perfectly, and the rare smile of approval that made her heart swell with pride.

                                                                                                                                                  The chastity belt, once a source of discomfort, became a symbol of her commitment. Her gloved fingers traced its edges, feeling the cool, unbreakable metal that both protected and confined her. Its pressure against her skin provided a constant reminder of her devotion. The knowledge that she couldn’t touch herself, even if she wanted to, gave her a new sense of restraint, making her feel both safe and vulnerable.

                                                                                                                                                  As the weeks turned into months, Sonia found comfort in her routine. Her world narrowed to Henry, the villa, and her duties. Her work at GAIA became secondary, an interruption of her time as a maid, where she could fully lose herself in service. She found satisfaction in the simplicity of her tasks, in the structure of her role. The chastity belt became a badge of honor, symbolizing her dedication.

                                                                                                                                                  She learned to treasure moments of approval, rare rewards, and the sense of belonging she felt. Her own desires faded, replaced by the desire to serve Henry and make his life better. Her submission didn’t take away from her—it completed her. It made her Henry’s. In that, she found true fulfillment.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia still had the vial Anna had made for her, the so-called “cure” for her incomplete infection. Anna had handed it to her with a simple message: “If you’re ready to accept the peace of the Changed, this will ‘fix’ you. Swallow it, and in two hours, you’ll be healed.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia kept the vial as a reminder of the path she could have chosen. But now, in her modest maid’s chamber next to Henry’s room, she realized she had found her own peace. She no longer needed the vial. She had found harmony within herself, discovering fulfillment and purpose in her submission to Henry.

                                                                                                                                                  The next day, Sonia met Anna at the Lakeside Café. Anna was dressed simply but stylishly—a flowing skirt, a tailored blouse, and a hijab covering her hair. They sat with their backs to the room, removed their masks, and donned veils that allowed them to share a coffee together. Anna’s eyes sparkled with curiosity.

                                                                                                                                                  “Sonia, it’s so good to see you again,” Anna signed warmly.

                                                                                                                                                  “It’s wonderful to see you too, Anna,” Sonia hand-danced back, pulling out the small vial and placing it on the table. Anna’s eyes widened.

                                                                                                                                                  “You still have it,” Anna signed, clearly surprised.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia nodded. “I kept it, just in case. But I don’t need it anymore. The serum is probably too old to work, but even if it wasn’t, I don’t need it.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna looked at her with understanding. “What changed?”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia took a deep breath. “When you gave me the vial, I was lost. I didn’t know how to navigate this new world. But then Henry came into my life, and everything changed.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna listened closely.

                                                                                                                                                  “Henry taught me to understand myself and embrace my nature. I thought submission made me weak, but he showed me it was the greatest gift I could offer. He guided me, corrected me, and helped me find my place in this world.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia’s signs became more confident. “Eventually, I realized I had ‘fixed’ myself. I found harmony within.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna took Sonia’s hand and squeezed it gently. “I’m so proud of you, Sonia. You’ve found your path, your peace.”

                                                                                                                                                  Tears welled up in Sonia’s eyes. “Thank you, Anna. You’ve always been there for me. I don’t need the vial anymore because I’ve found what I was looking for.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna smiled, her eyes moist as well. “We’ve both found our places in this world. That’s something to celebrate.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia nodded, feeling deep gratitude and love for her friend. “Yes, we have. And I’m grateful for every step of the journey that brought us here.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia knew she had found her place, her purpose, and her peace. A deep sense of contentment settled within her.
