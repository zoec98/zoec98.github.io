                                                                                                                                                  Chapter 20: Decoding the Virus

                                                                                                                                                  Decoding the Virus#

                                                                                                                                                  During the following days, Anna continued her analysis of the files Sonia had brought from Shanghai, matching the data Sonia brought her with the analysis of the genome she had so far. Reengaging with her passion for genetics within her new lifestyle, Anna worked tirelessly through days and nights. Once she had some results, she was eager to discuss her findings with Sonia, and they met in Anna’s underground lab.

                                                                                                                                                  Her hands dancing, Anna began to explain with scientific precision her findings to Sonia. “The virus is a masterpiece of genetic engineering. It’s airborne, highly contagious, and highly efficient at crossing the blood-brain barrier. It doesn’t just infect; it rewrites and imprints new memories and values directly into the brain’s neural pathways,” Anna explained, her admiration for the design evident.

                                                                                                                                                  “This way,” she continued, “it doesn’t force its new behaviors on the person. Instead, they are remembered, and the human brain wraps them in existing memories of how these behaviors were learned, modifying existing memories to fit. Changed have age-old traditions, rules and rituals, because they remember these things as something they have always had. At the same time, the old memories are forgotten, and the fact that the old behaviors and rules are seen as shameful helps a lot.”

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia listened intently as Anna continued. “The genome is predominantly data, an imprint. Additionally, it contains compulsory behaviors. They are tailored specifically to affect the infected based on their sex. The compulsion to serve and submit is activated only in females. Males are affected by a different set of values and compulsions,” Anna explained, simultaneously signing a mix of professionalism and awe.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna showed Sonia the data on her laptop, pointing out the important parts. She moved easily, still a skilled scientist, but her signing showed she had accepted her new life. “It’s… elegant, the way it brings harmony, order. I couldn’t have designed it better myself,” Anna admitted, her gaze meeting Sonia’s. They were very different now—Anna admired the virus, and Sonia wanted to fight it.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia experienced a whirlwind of emotions. The virus was complex and deeply integrated into society, but Anna’s insights offered a glimmer of hope. They could use Anna’s discoveries to combat the virus. Realizing that Anna could still utilize her scientific expertise was crucial, and Sonia saw it as a vital tool in their fight. Faced with numerous challenges, Sonia felt emboldened by the new knowledge and Anna’s support. Together, she resolved to find a way to undermine the virus’s influence with Anna’s help, targeting one genetic sequence at a time.
