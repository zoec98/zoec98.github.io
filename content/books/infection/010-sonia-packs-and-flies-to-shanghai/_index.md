# Sonia packs and flies to Shanghai

Arriving at GAIA, Sonia did not go straight upstairs.
She said goodbye to Anna, who continued up to her office and stopped in the GAIA lobby and decided to do some science: Changed vs. Unchanged numbers.

Changed women were the easiest to spot: gloves, fitted skirts, high collars, scarves or hats, the same contained economy of movement she had already started to recognize. Changed men took longer. Their clothes varied less, but the posture gave them away sooner or later. A new stillness. More attention to the women with them. An altered kind of ease in taking up space.

Fifteen minutes gave her enough for a working sample.

Roughly one person in six.

Sick leave reports would push the real number higher. She caught fragments as people crossed the lobby in pairs and threes: another lab down three staff, someone still feverish, someone else's wife suddenly refusing trousers, a senior researcher "not herself at all" and therefore, in the speaker's opinion, doing wonderfully.

The virus was already everywhere inside the institute, not an isolated incident, and it would not simply stop. Even the Unchanged would be turned soon.

Sonia closed the notebook and went upstairs.

Her office felt smaller in the blouse and skirt. The gloves had to come off before she could type properly, which meant working the cuffs loose and swearing under her breath at tiny buttons meant for more patient hands. The Yoyah Sheengzoh kept asserting itself even seated. It did not let her spread out. It organized her knees, her hips, the angle of her back.

Useful to know. Infuriating to experience.

She started with the data she trusted most: spread curves, outbreak timing, internal reports from partner hospitals, early sample metadata, shipping records tied to the first clusters. That work she understood. The other line she built next was messier and stranger.

Boutiques.

She pinned dates to a wall timeline until the pattern stopped looking like coincidence. Outbreak, then Maylee Shufoo. Another outbreak, another boutique. Not always the same week or the same district, but close enough to stop being funny.

The boutiques were not a side effect.

They were part of the same machine.

Sonia sat back and rubbed at one temple.

Viruses did not sign leases or register shell companies. People did.

She went hunting for the people.

Maylee Shufoo in Geneva led to a registration. The registration led to Veridis Quo. Veridis Quo led to Maximilian Grothe, who existed on paper and almost nowhere else. Drexel and Saigne LLC appeared in the margins through consulting records, asset-protection language, restructuring filings, boring corporate language wrapped around something rotten. Sonia could follow enough of it to know she was out of depth and still nowhere near the actual hand on the wheel.

She reached for her phone.

Mara Delacroix picked up on the second ring.

"Tell me this is billable."

"Almost certainly," Sonia said.

"Good. Then I can still like you. What happened?"

"I need ownership tracing on a retail chain with layered shell structures and probably offshore nonsense. Fast."

There was a brief silence.

"That does sound like me," Mara said. "How fast?"

"Yesterday."

"Lunch. Twenty minutes. Bring whatever you've got."

The line went dead.

That was why Sonia called her.

Mara arrived two minutes late and made lateness look intentional. Small, sharp, dark suit, no wasted motion, no softness anywhere that had not been selected for effect. She slid into the chair opposite Sonia, set down her phone, and looked once at the folder Sonia had brought.

"You look absurd," Mara said.

"Thank you."

"You know I mean the clothes."

"I assumed so."

Mara's mouth twitched.

"Good. I was worried Geneva had finally bullied you into participating."

Sonia pushed the papers across the table.

"Not yet."

Mara scanned the first page and stilled.

"Maylee Shufoo."

"You know them?"

"Everyone knows them. The retro-submission boutique for women who want to dress like a Stepford wives cosplay."

Sonia almost smiled despite herself.

"Useful. Keep going."

Mara flipped through the registrations, slower now.

"Veridis Quo is real enough. Grothe is probably a mask. Drexel and Saigne I've seen before, not on anything clean. They're the kind of consultants people hire when they want three extra walls between themselves and liability."

"Can you break it open?"

Mara looked up.

"Of course I can break it open. The question is how much crime you want me to imply while I do it."

"Only the amount that's already there."

"Reasonable."

She kept reading.

"Why do you care?"

Sonia took a sip of the terrible coffee and gave her the version that was mostly true.

"We're tracking unusual overlap between the boutiques and a viral event. I want the ownership map before I decide whether that overlap is coincidence, opportunism, or design."

Mara glanced again at Sonia's collar, gloves, skirt.

"And you are wearing their product because?"

"Field camouflage."

"You hate it."

"Deeply."

"Good," Mara said. "I'd worry otherwise."

She stacked the papers into a neater pile than Sonia had managed.

"I'll send you corporate trees, registrations, beneficial ownership if I can get it, and anything interesting buried under the consultant layer. If someone in this chain is trying to stay invisible, I want to know what they were afraid of before they had the good sense to be afraid."

"Mara."

"Yes?"

"Thank you."

That earned Sonia a dismissive wave.

"Buy me something expensive when this turns into a disaster."

By the next morning Mara had sent thirty-seven files and a message with no greeting.

`You were right to ask. Start with folders 3, 5, and 9. Someone is hiding on purpose.`

Sonia read standing up.

Mara had pulled the whole structure into the light. Veridis Quo fed into overseas entities. Drexel and Saigne sat between clusters of holdings like reactor shielding. Maylee Shufoo linked outward to another chain Sonia had never heard of before seeing the documents:

迷人的景象

"Mirende Ginciao", if read aloud from what the virus had installed in her.
"A Captivating Sight."

The American-facing name, buried in a market report, was Mirren's.

Same product logic. Same aesthetics with regional tweaks. Same timing near infection zones. Same corporate fog.

And all the cleanest lines led back to Shanghai.

Sonia's pulse steadied instead of jumping. Good. A city. A direction. Something you could board a plane toward.

She sent messages into the contacts she still had from prior China trips. Journalist. Founder. One old research liaison. Most answers came back thin or useless. People had heard rumors. People knew someone who knew someone. People asked why she cared.

The useful response came late and without charm from Jiang Han, who called himself a broker because any more specific noun would have sounded criminal.

> You want the man behind Cheng Biotech Industries. I can get you enough to justify a visit. Not enough to keep you safe.

Sonia wrote back at once.

> Send it.

The dossier landed twenty minutes later.

Dr. Mingyu Cheng. "ABC", American-born Chinese. Family money. Neurological biotech. CBI founded in the nineties, respectable in exactly the way that meant dangerous if the respectability ever cracked. He had patents, board memberships, old press, vanished collaborators, and a private residence in Shanghai straight out of a Bond film.

Sonia read the file twice.

Neurological disorders.

Of course.

If half the world had not already started dressing its women like managed relics, the whole thing would have sounded melodramatic. As it was, she only felt grimly confirmed.

Shanghai was no longer a hypothesis. It was the next step.

That part required Clara.

Sonia drafted the field-trip request with careful restraint. Sample tracing. Early cluster analysis. Origin mapping. Existing contacts on the ground. Shenzhen precedent. No mention of boutiques, shell companies, or the possibility that a biotech magnate was underwriting social engineering through couture.

She sent it, then carried the same controlled version of herself into her one-on-one with Clara Dumont.

Clara was Changed.

Sonia knew that before she consciously registered the details. Then the details arrived all at once: the gray skirt too narrow for a natural stride, the blouse buttoned high and closed at the back, the hijab, the satin gloves resting on the desk as if they belonged there. Clara had always been authoritative. Now the authority had been rerouted through a version of femininity that should have weakened it and somehow did not.

Sonia sat carefully.

"You wanted to discuss Shanghai," Clara said.

Her tone was efficient. Calm. Helpful.

Too helpful.

"Yes," Sonia said. "I want to trace early infection clusters through existing research contacts and collect additional samples. The Shenzhen trip gave us enough structure to justify a second field pass."

Clara opened the proposal and read more quickly than Sonia expected.

"This makes sense."

That was it. No resistance. No budget objection. No concern about timeline, exposure, biohazard transport, duplicated effort, or Sonia's suddenly corrected clothes.

Sonia waited for the harder part.

It did not come.

"You should go," Clara said. "Sooner rather than later."

Sonia kept her face neutral.

"You agree?"

Clara looked up, and for one strange second she seemed almost warmly pleased.

"Of course. If there is useful data to gather, you should gather it."

The answer was reasonable. That was what made it wrong.

Sonia had worked under Clara for years. Pre-Change Clara would have asked six harder questions before offering support, then three more while pretending she had already decided. This Clara approved the trip as if helping Sonia do difficult work gave her genuine satisfaction.

Eerie.

It was not blank obedience or mindless enthusiasm, but something smoother, cooperative at the root.

"I can authorize travel this afternoon," Clara said. "Do you need anything else?"

There it was again. Readiness. Support. No visible defensiveness even though the research Sonia was proposing might eventually circle back to the thing that had remade her.

"No," Sonia said. "That should be enough."

"Good. Keep me informed."

Sonia left with the approval in hand and a fresh problem she could not yet name.

Why were the Changed so willing to help?

Not just Anna. Clara too. Not eager to be changed back, not horrified, not pleading for a cure, and still cooperative with an investigation that might threaten the system that now held them.

She did not have time to solve that before boarding a plane.

So she packed.

Passport. Existing Chinese paperwork. Biohazard handling documents. Work visa still valid. Chinese phone, active SIM, spare battery pack, encrypted drive, sample tubes, laptop, chargers, hard copies of the Cheng file, harder copies of Mara's ownership maps. One small medical kit. One knife she would not be allowed to keep past security and packed anyway before remembering herself.

Then clothes.

That part stopped her.

She could fly in jeans and draw eyes the moment she landed, or she could travel as the city now expected women to travel, not because she had yielded, but because she needed to move.

Tactical. Temporary. Useful.

The lie was getting more efficient every time she used it.

She went back to Maylee Shufoo before leaving for the airport.

Now that she knew the chain belonged in the same file as the virus, she saw the place differently: infrastructure, public-facing doctrine, a showroom for the society the virus was building inside people.

The new stock had shifted again.

Corsets now, more than before. Stiffer skirts. Narrower sleeves. High-necked blouses that looked severe even on mannequins. One new line caught her at once:

高抱佑护

"Gowbao Yoohoo," the installed Chinese offered.
"Embrace and Protect."

The blouse was a beautiful trap. Pale fabric, severe line, immaculate finish. Inside the collar sat a reinforced leather structure that would hold the neck high and still once fastened. The sleeves buttoned along the outer forearm in a way that made self-dressing clumsy at best. Gloves would make it worse. The whole design implied assistance, supervision, hands other than the wearer's.

The name pretended tenderness.

Protect.
Embrace.

It really meant "hold still."

Sonia touched the collar once with two fingertips and imagined it closed around a woman's throat, posture corrected, gaze managed, dependence built directly into dress.

The boutiques were teaching the same lesson as the virus, not through genes, but through ritual, inconvenience, beauty, public expectation, and the slow conversion of care into control.

She bought nothing else.

At the airport she moved with the same shortened stride she despised, documents in order, her anger folded back so tightly it almost passed for calm.

Shanghai next.

That was good enough.
