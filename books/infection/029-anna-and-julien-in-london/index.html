                                                                                                                                                    Anna and Julien in London#

                                                                                                                                                    London began with rain on brick and the feeling that the city had been designed by people who regarded weather as a moral fact.

                                                                                                                                                    The B&B on Great Titchfield Street was narrower than Anna had imagined and more ingenious. Brick exterior, polished brass, a small ground-floor boutique, a pair of upper rooms given over to guests, and everywhere little systems of accommodation shaped specifically around the Changed life. Nothing announced itself as spectacular. That was the point. The city did not think restraint was exotic. It thought it was infrastructure.

                                                                                                                                                    Mike Smith greeted them first, broad-shouldered, dryly cheerful, already reaching for the luggage. His Wendy followed a step later, practical and warm in the distinctly British way that made efficiency sound like kindness. She wore a belted rain cape over a structured dress, gloves, a hat with layered veils, and the visible rail attachment at the back of her corseted waist as casually as a woman elsewhere might wear a handbag strap.

                                                                                                                                                    Anna noticed all of it at once.

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy noticed that she noticed.

                                                                                                                                                    “First time in London?” she asked.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien answered for both of them.

                                                                                                                                                    “Yes.”

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy smiled at Anna through the veil.

                                                                                                                                                    “You’ll either find it absurd or wonderfully sensible by tomorrow.”

                                                                                                                                                    Anna smiled behind her veil.

                                                                                                                                                    “Perhaps both.”

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy laughed softly.

                                                                                                                                                    “That is usually the correct answer.”

                                                                                                                                                    Their suite occupied the upper floor and looked out over wet rooftops, chimney pots, and a surprising amount of green. The rooftop garden Wendy had mentioned in her message was real, not decorative, and still visible even in the rain: raised beds, herbs, a small water catchment system, careful use of every piece of space.

                                                                                                                                                    Inside, the more revealing details emerged.

                                                                                                                                                    The rail system ran along the ceiling of the bedroom and sitting room both, with a clean exterior track visible outside the window, linking the domestic interior to the street below. Dock points had been built into furniture, not as curiosities but as expected conveniences. There were hooks placed exactly where hats, veils, muffs, and capes would need to be managed. A standing support near the dressing mirror suggested that no one in the house considered female dressing entirely independent work.

                                                                                                                                                    Anna stood in the middle of the room and turned slowly, looking upward.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien watched her take it in.

                                                                                                                                                    “Overwhelmed?”

                                                                                                                                                    “Interested.”

                                                                                                                                                    That answer pleased him too much to hide.

                                                                                                                                                    They unpacked, and then Julien told her to change for the boutique downstairs because he wanted their first outing to be simple. Shopping. Looking. Learning local standards before they tried to perform them in public.

                                                                                                                                                    The boutique beneath the B&B sold weather, modesty, and dependence with remarkable elegance.

                                                                                                                                                    Latex catsuits in varying weights hung beside walking skirts, structured capes, fitted rain hoods, veiled hats, lockable muffs, and rainproof oversleeves that narrowed the arms into decorative helplessness while still claiming practicality. The colors were more disciplined than Geneva’s. Black, cream, storm blue, muted teal, signal orange used almost clinically. British taste seemed less interested in prettiness than in surface, polish, and visible engineering.

                                                                                                                                                    Anna loved it immediately.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien put her through the process methodically.

                                                                                                                                                    The thin black catsuit first, because it would show him the cut. Then a thicker muted teal one, slick against her skin and just resistant enough at the joints to make every movement seem already supervised. Then the orange one, more aggressively restrictive, which made her laugh softly behind the veil when she tried to bend and discovered how much of the decision had already been made for her by the material.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien’s eyes darkened in a way she recognized.

                                                                                                                                                    “That one later,” he said.

                                                                                                                                                    Anna lowered her gaze.

                                                                                                                                                    “Yes, Sir.”

                                                                                                                                                    They chose three in total, each for a different weather and visibility problem. Then came the real British refinements.

                                                                                                                                                    The tailor downstairs examined Anna’s existing corsets and proposed rail adaptations that could be fixed discreetly into them: attach points between the shoulder blades and at the small of the back, depending on outerwear and destination. He discussed them with Julien the way one might discuss luggage handles or boot soles.

                                                                                                                                                    Anna listened with growing fascination while locked to the small fitting dais with her hands arranged and her veils still in place.

                                                                                                                                                    This was what London seemed to do best. It took female dependence and moved it out of the realm of embarrassment. It treated it as a design category.

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy joined them midway through the fitting with a tray of tea, as if she had wandered in by chance and not because British hospitality required a competent woman to appear at exactly the useful moment.

                                                                                                                                                    “If you’re going to be out in weather,” she said, setting the tray down, “get the proper capes and don’t be silly about it. Tourists always try to compromise. Then their women end up cold, wet, or badly guided.”

                                                                                                                                                    Anna looked at her.

                                                                                                                                                    “Badly guided?”

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy nodded as if the phrase were entirely ordinary.

                                                                                                                                                    “Of course. Rain makes everyone hurry. That is when systems matter most.”

                                                                                                                                                    She tapped one of the lockable muffs with one gloved finger.

                                                                                                                                                    “These seem severe until you’ve crossed Regent Street in weather and realized your hands would only have been useless outside anyway.”

                                                                                                                                                    Anna adored her a little for saying it that way.

                                                                                                                                                    They left the boutique with capes, muffs, adapted corsets, and the first beginnings of an English wardrobe. By then the rain had thickened into a steady silver fall over the street outside.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien fastened her into one of the lighter setups before they went up: catsuit, walking skirt, fitted cape, veiled hat, and one of the muffs belted at her waist. He did it without hurry, right there in the private fitting space behind the shop, as if the process itself were part of introducing her properly to the city.

                                                                                                                                                    When he finished and stepped back, Anna could feel what had changed immediately.

                                                                                                                                                    Geneva had arranged her into correctness. London arranged her into managed weatherproof display.

                                                                                                                                                    She could walk. She could not hurry. She could see. She could not see widely. She could travel. She would not travel unassisted.

                                                                                                                                                    Julien looked her over once and then asked the only question that mattered.

                                                                                                                                                    “Well?”

                                                                                                                                                    Anna answered with a satisfaction so deep it almost felt like gratitude.

                                                                                                                                                    “It is practical in a way that admits exactly what it is.”

                                                                                                                                                    Wendy, who had been pretending not to watch, gave a small approving sound.

                                                                                                                                                    “Yes,” she said. “You’ve understood us already.”

                                                                                                                                                    That evening, after supper and after the rain had deepened the city into reflected gold and black outside their window, Anna stood beneath the ceiling rail in the suite and let Julien attach the newly adapted corset point into the indoor system just to feel how the house wanted her.

                                                                                                                                                    The connection was light, almost gentle.

                                                                                                                                                    The meaning of it was not.

                                                                                                                                                    She looked out over wet London and felt, not fear, but excitement sharpen into something calmer and more enduring. This city had not invented what she already was. It had simply externalized it, built it into walls, weather, clothing, and movement until dependence could travel openly as care.
