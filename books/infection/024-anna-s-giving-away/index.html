                                                                                                                                                  Chapter 24: Anna's Giving-Away

                                                                                                                                                  • Anna’s Giving-Away

                                                                                                                                                  Anna’s Giving-Away#

                                                                                                                                                  On the morning of her Giving-Away, Anna was in her bridal suite, filled with quiet excitement. “Let’s begin, Anna,” Claudette, her dresser and makeup artist, signed.

                                                                                                                                                  She first helped Anna into a delicate undergarment, setting the day’s traditional tone. Then, Claudette fitted Anna with a corset, shaping her figure into the accepted ideal. With each iteration of the lacing, the pressure around her waist grew, a reminder of the commitment and submission she was about to pledge. Anna breathed deeply, adjusting to the corset’s embrace, finding peace in the restriction while Claudette adjusted the shoulder braces. The Yoyah Sheengzoh underskirt followed, restricting Anna’s steps, forcing her into the glide that society expected from her.

                                                                                                                                                  For makeup, Claudette applied a look to match Anna’s white porcelain mask, a Jurenmuse with a doll-face design. This mask, with its demanding gag, would be the final touch, a barrier between Anna and the world that left her visible only through the narrow window of her eyes.

                                                                                                                                                  Claudette then braided Anna’s hair into a single braid, rolling it up and pinning it into a bun at the back of her head to fit it under her chosen headscarf, ensuring elegance and modesty were maintained. The scarf was chosen to match the dress, red with golden patterns for luck and success, securing her hair and ensuring that not a single lock was out of place.

                                                                                                                                                  By nine in the morning, Anna was fully dressed in her modernized hanfu, embodying the world’s values she had chosen to embrace. The heels she wore added to her refined appearance, supported by the corset and underskirt. In the mirror, Anna saw a bride ready to fulfill her role in a world of order and harmony.

                                                                                                                                                  “Thank you, Claudette,” Anna signed, feeling the significance of her transformation. Claudette curtsied and smiled, acknowledging her part in preparing Anna for this significant day. After Claudette left, Anna stood alone for a moment, looking into the mirror.

                                                                                                                                                  Then she let Sonia in, who arrived at the agreed time. Once inside the bridal suite, Sonia removed her mask with the complicated movements necessary to get the shield out, and put it aside. She then had a look at Anna in her extremely demanding dress, her face hidden behind the white porcelain mask. Sonia was experiencing very mixed emotions as she observed Anna turning to the mirror, rehearsing her wedding vows to Julien once again. Her hands moved gracefully, signing her commitment and love. “Julien,” she began, her fingers crafting his name in the air, “as I stand before you, I embrace not just you but the world we are stepping into together.” Anna’s hands danced through her promises, each movement a silent word.

                                                                                                                                                  She signed about embracing the womanly virtues, promising grace, humility, and righteousness. Anna’s hands told the story of shifting her loyalty from her father to Julien, a significant change. “Today, as I transition my obedience to you, I do so with a heart full of trust and commitment,” she conveyed through her hands.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna pledged her support and submission, a commitment made in the silence of hand-dancing. As she finished, she felt ready and at peace, despite the mask separating her from the world.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia asked about Julien’s vows. Anna knew them, and showed them to her: He would be promising to uphold their sacred principles and to extend his deep respect to their marriage. “By accepting your obedience, I realize its importance and promise to honor it,” she pictured him saying. Julien would vow to protect and support Anna. For her, their vows were not just words but a bond, a life built on tradition and mutual respect. Julien’s commitment made her feel valued and loved.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna knelt down in front of the mirror, looking at herself, ready to commit to her new life with Julien. Sonia stood by and watched as Anna started her special ritual, using hand-dancing to express her mantras, her commitments, an exercise she would be doing now every morning at the start of her day.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will embrace silence to better honor my role,” her hands conveyed, a gesture toward the mask covering her face. This piece symbolized her vow of submission and obedience, a tangible reminder of her commitment to speak only when it serves harmony and to let her actions speak louder than her words.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will practice humility to better serve our union,” she signed. This mantra tied closely to the simple elegance of her dress, chosen for its reflection of traditional values, embodying the modesty and grace expected of her in her new life.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will cultivate patience to better support Julien’s guidance,” Anna continued. The restrictive nature of her corset served as the anchor for this ideal, a constant, physical reminder of the constraints within which she would find her strength and purpose.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will seek his wisdom to better understand my duties,” her hands articulated, touching the fabric of her headscarf gently. The scarf represented the pursuit of knowledge within her prescribed role, a commitment to learning and adapting within the framework of her new responsibilities.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will demonstrate diligence to better uphold our household,” she signed. Each piece of her wedding attire, carefully chosen and worn, was a symbol of her dedication to her role.

                                                                                                                                                  With each mantra, Anna embraced the Changed ideals, seeing them as paths to fulfillment and peace, not restrictions. This silent ritual was a promise for her future, a daily reminder of her role and responsibilities in a world seeking harmony.

                                                                                                                                                  As she finished, Anna felt peaceful, looking at herself in the mirror. The connection between her ideals and her dress made her role clear. The depth of Anna’s submission and commitment left Sonia shaken. Every time she thought she had a grasp on Changed society, something happened and showed her how different the virus had made the Changed from… well, from herself. There were hardly any Unchanged people left.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna turned to Sonia and signed in graceful motions: “The person I was before the Change… was ignorant and rebellious against the harmony of our world,” she expressed.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia wanted to argue, was about to raise her voice, but Anna stopped her, signaling her to use hand-dancing too. Sonia tried, apologizing for this breach of Changed etiquette.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna told Sonia she had a choice to make, picking up her point from the conversation they had at the Lakeside Café. “You, like the old me, face a choice. Not about whom you will become—society has already outlined our roles, especially for women, and Changed society leaves little room here, as you have already experienced. It’s about accepting your place, willingly, or through the aid of the vial I gave you.”

                                                                                                                                                  The vial. Sonia had put it into her refrigerator at home, and vowed to herself that she would never touch it. But Anna had a point. The pressure was real. She had felt it in her months as a maid in Dr. Cheng’s household, with its rigid discipline, the enforced anonymity and the pressure to submit and obey. She was also feeling it here, every day, whenever she interacted with any Changed. Changed society was more rigid and harsh, in certain ways, than Sonia ever imagined. Individualism had its place in Changed society only as long as people stayed inside their assigned roles, playing their part in the greater orchestrated play that Changed society was. They called it Harmony, but it was that only from a distance.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia wrestled the gag of her mask back into her mouth, biting down and arranging her hijab. Her hands thanked Anna for what she had shown her, for everything really. She then wished her a happy and successful marriage. Sonia was unsure if she should be envious. If she could find herself being happy in such a deep submission. Then she left to mingle with the arriving guests, all of them Changed and sure, oh so sure, about their role and place in the world.
