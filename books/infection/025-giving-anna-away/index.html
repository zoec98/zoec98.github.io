                                                                                                                                                  Chapter 25: Giving Anna Away

                                                                                                                                                  Giving Anna Away#

                                                                                                                                                  By noon the Jade Garden had filled with the kind of quiet company Anna had wanted from the beginning.

                                                                                                                                                  No crowd. No spectacle for its own sake. Only a small assembly of family, close friends, and the people whose presence mattered to the shape of the vow. The pond reflected pale light through the carved screens. Gravel paths curved between clipped pines and dark wood. Everywhere the garden offered calm, and everywhere the calm sharpened the fact that something irreversible was about to happen.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia stood among the guests in proper dress and mask and watched the place take on ceremony.

                                                                                                                                                  Women stood or sat with the particular composure formal clothes and formal expectation drew out of them. Men occupied space more openly, greeting one another, adjusting details, guiding older relatives into place. A low platform had been arranged near the water with a tea table already set behind it, cups waiting under linen covers for later.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien arrived before Anna.

                                                                                                                                                  He wore dark formal dress with Chinese details worked into the cut, the result restrained enough not to compete with the bride but deliberate enough to show that he understood exactly what this day meant. He greeted the guests, spoke quietly with staff, exchanged a few final words with Marc Lefevre, and then, for a brief interval before the formal beginning, withdrew with Anna into one of the garden’s side rooms.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia did not follow. She saw enough through the half-open screen before it slid shut to understand the shape of it.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna in white porcelain and red silk. Julien bending his head to see what she signed. His hand resting once at her waist, not possessive for display, only steadying. Her body answering that touch with visible ease.

                                                                                                                                                  The screen closed.

                                                                                                                                                  The guests turned their attention toward the platform.

                                                                                                                                                  When Anna emerged at last, led by her father, the entire garden seemed to settle around the sight of her.

                                                                                                                                                  The white Jurenmuse erased her mouth and left only her eyes to speak. The red and gold of the dress moved richly around the severe calm of the mask. Her corseted waist and constrained stride made every step measured enough that Marc naturally moderated his own pace to match hers. He did not look burdened by that. He looked proud.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia felt a strange hollow open in her chest as she watched them approach.

                                                                                                                                                  Not because the beauty disguised the patriarchy.

                                                                                                                                                  Because the beauty made the patriarchy feel emotionally legible from the inside.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc brought Anna to the center of the platform and turned to face her first, before facing Julien.

                                                                                                                                                  That detail mattered. Sonia saw it at once. The ceremony was not only transfer. It was release.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien stepped forward and stopped at the appropriate distance. The guests fell into complete silence. Even the water at the pond edge seemed to lower its voice.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc lifted one hand.

                                                                                                                                                  “Anna.”

                                                                                                                                                  His voice was formal, but tenderness moved beneath it plainly enough for everyone to hear.

                                                                                                                                                  “You come before me as my daughter and ward, raised under my house and my authority. Do you come in gratitude and good conduct, ready to be released honorably from my care?”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna’s gloved hands rose.

                                                                                                                                                  Yes, Sir. In gratitude I come. For your care, your guidance, and the honor of your house, I give thanks. I ask to be released from your authority with your blessing.

                                                                                                                                                  The sight of the white porcelain while those signs moved in the air gave the moment an eerie kind of purity Sonia would have despised more easily if it had not also been beautiful.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc watched every sign.

                                                                                                                                                  “You have honored my house well,” he said. “I release you freely and with pride.”

                                                                                                                                                  That was the first severing.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia felt it like a thread cut in public.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc stepped aside half a pace then, not abandoning Anna but no longer standing as the man who defined her. He turned toward Julien.

                                                                                                                                                  “Julien Moreau, do you come prepared to take this woman into your care and answer for her before your house, your family, and your society?”

                                                                                                                                                  “I do.”

                                                                                                                                                  Julien’s voice carried cleanly across the platform. He did not raise it. He did not need to.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc looked back to Anna.

                                                                                                                                                  “Then go to him.”

                                                                                                                                                  Anna moved.

                                                                                                                                                  The dress made it a glide rather than a walk. Short steps, composed balance, hands gathered before her until she reached Julien and lowered herself into a kneel that the clothing turned into something difficult enough to be meaningful. Sonia saw one of the older women in the guest row lower her eyes at the sight, moved almost to tears.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna lifted her hands.

                                                                                                                                                  Sir, if willing, take me into your care.

                                                                                                                                                  The signs were flawless.

                                                                                                                                                  To you, I offer loyalty, obedience, and faithful conduct.

                                                                                                                                                  Your guidance, I will honor, and your house, I will uphold with diligence and grace.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien looked down at her for one suspended heartbeat, and Sonia saw what the scene would feel like from Anna’s side. Exposure. Relief. Completion. The erotic danger of asking and waiting to be accepted.

                                                                                                                                                  Then he extended his hand.

                                                                                                                                                  “I will have you.”

                                                                                                                                                  He did not lift her immediately. He gave the words their full shape first.

                                                                                                                                                  “I take you into my care before these witnesses. I promise you protection, guidance, and faithful regard. I will answer for your well-being, honor your trust, and keep you under my protection as befits my ward and wife.”

                                                                                                                                                  Only then did he take her hand and bring her to her feet.

                                                                                                                                                  When Anna rose, it was no longer as Marc Lefevre’s Anna.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc spoke the change aloud for everyone.

                                                                                                                                                  “Then let it be known that this woman leaves my authority with blessing and enters yours with honor. She is no longer of my name. She stands before us now as Julien Moreau’s Anna.”

                                                                                                                                                  The phrase moved through the gathering like a physical thing.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia hated how much power it had.

                                                                                                                                                  She hated, more, that Anna visibly loved hearing it.

                                                                                                                                                  Even behind the mask, that was clear. In the way her body settled. In the tiny softening of her shoulders under the braces. In the almost imperceptible lean toward Julien as if the naming itself had given her somewhere to rest.

                                                                                                                                                  The guests bowed their heads or murmured polite assent. A few of the women signed blessings too quickly and lightly for the men to read in detail. Marc stepped back completely then, his role concluded.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien and Anna turned together toward the tea table.

                                                                                                                                                  This was where the ritual became domestic, and therefore more intimate.

                                                                                                                                                  They took their places on opposite sides of the low table. Anna had to lower herself carefully again, the dress and underskirt making even that one act a small public display of constraint. Julien watched without helping because the ceremony required the effort to be visible. Only once she was settled did he sit opposite her.

                                                                                                                                                  The tea set had been chosen with almost absurd care. White porcelain edged in deep green. Small cups. Narrow pot. A lacquer tray beneath it all reflecting sleeve, hand, silk, and sky in dark blurred shapes.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna began with the cleansing.

                                                                                                                                                  She lifted the pot and cups, rinsed them, poured out the first water, then warmed the porcelain with steady gloved hands. Nothing in the sequence was rushed. Sonia had expected more abstraction from a public tea ritual. Instead it was bodily and exact. The turn of Anna’s wrist. The drag of the sleeve. The slight pause required when the corset limited her lean and forced her to redistribute effort through shoulders and forearms.

                                                                                                                                                  Constraint made the ritual more visible.

                                                                                                                                                  Not less.

                                                                                                                                                  When she measured the leaves, Anna’s hands were so steady they looked serene.

                                                                                                                                                  The water followed. The wait. The pour.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien watched her with the full attention of a man being publicly served by a woman who had just asked to be governed by him, and the concentration in his face kept the scene from slipping into pretty pageantry. He understood that the tea was not decorative. She was giving him her discipline, her composure, her readiness to serve in a form everyone present could witness and read.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna lifted the first cup and offered it across the table.

                                                                                                                                                  Both hands. Lowered gaze. Perfect stillness.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien received it the same way a man might receive an oath.

                                                                                                                                                  “Thank you.”

                                                                                                                                                  The words were quiet. They landed like praise anyway.

                                                                                                                                                  He drank.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia watched Anna feel it.

                                                                                                                                                  The reaction was small, private, unmistakable. The little bodily yielding that came whenever male approval reached the place the virus had prepared for it. This was not theory anymore. It was in front of her, in silk and porcelain and sunlight.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna prepared the second cup after that and drank from it herself, completing the exchange. Then the sequence continued in smaller formal movements: the arrangement of the cups, the shared pour, the symbolic joining not through a kiss or a modern declaration of equality but through service accepted and protection implied in that acceptance.

                                                                                                                                                  Somewhere behind Sonia, someone sniffed softly.

                                                                                                                                                  When the formal sequence ended, applause came not as a roar but as a swelling of contained approval through the garden. Gloves touching lightly. Men nodding. Women signing blessings to one another. Staff moving in silent competence at the edges. The whole setting seemed to breathe out.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien rose first and offered Anna his hand.

                                                                                                                                                  She took it.

                                                                                                                                                  This time when she stood, he did help. He steadied the difficult upward movement her dress demanded, and that simple assistance, coming after the public transfer and the tea, seemed to seal the whole thing more deeply than any speech still could have.

                                                                                                                                                  Marc approached them once more, no longer as Anna’s authority, but as family.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien bowed his head respectfully. Anna lowered hers.

                                                                                                                                                  “You have both done honor to the day,” Marc said.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna signed her thanks. Julien answered aloud.

                                                                                                                                                  Around them the guests began to loosen into post-ceremony life. Tea for others. Soft conversation. The relief of people who had witnessed something emotionally satisfying and morally unquestioned by the culture around them.

                                                                                                                                                  Sonia stood among them and felt more apart than she had inside the bridal suite.

                                                                                                                                                  Before the ceremony, Anna had still been split between names, between houses, between male authorities. That intermediate state had left room for Sonia’s disbelief to catch on something human and unfinished. Now the ritual had done its work. Marc had set her free. Julien had taken her in. The old name had fallen away in public. The new one had been spoken aloud and answered by Anna’s whole body.

                                                                                                                                                  Julien Moreau’s Anna.

                                                                                                                                                  It should have sounded grotesque.

                                                                                                                                                  Instead, in that garden, with that woman, it sounded cherished.

                                                                                                                                                  That was the part Sonia could not forgive the world for making true.
