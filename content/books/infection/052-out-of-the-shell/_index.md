# Out of the Shell

Mara slowly opened her eyes, squinting against the harsh artificial light flooding the room.
The brightness stung, piercing her eyes,
which were unaccustomed to such intensity after so long in controlled environments.
She instinctively closed them again as a wave of disorientation and pain washed over her.

Everything hurt.

Panic rose inside her as she realized how vulnerable she was.
Her protective shell, the iGuardian that had encased her for years, was gone.
Her mouth felt strangely empty, her face bare and exposed, with nothing to shield her from the world.
Her hands, once always gloved, were now uncovered, as were her feet.
With a jolt, she realized she was completely naked,
covered only by a rough blanket that did little to protect her from the cold or the shame of her exposed state.
She felt dirty, as if something essential had been stripped away.

A rough, cracking sound escaped her throat as she tried to speak, but her vocal cords, unused for years, failed her.
The sound was more a groan than a word, raw and primal.
Whoever had done this had removed her iGuardian and everything else that had defined her existence,
leaving her in a strange, cold bed.
Her body betrayed her further, leaking uncontrollably now that the inserts that had regulated her were gone.
The muscles that had once held her in perfect posture now screamed in agony,
unable to perform even the simplest tasks without the support they had grown used to.

Through the haze of pain and fear, she heard a voice—Sonia’s voice, soft and close.

"Mara, are you awake?"
Sonia's voice was barely above a whisper, sending a shiver through Mara.
Sonia, too, must have been unmasked, vulnerable like Mara.

Mara managed a low, guttural moan in response, the sound rough and barely intelligible.
She couldn’t move much, every attempt to do so sending sharp pains through her body.
But she was used to pain; it had been a constant companion in her life, and now it served as a motivator.
She began to systematically test her body, trying to regain some control.
Fingers twitched, arms moved slightly, and her legs responded sluggishly.
Every motion was a struggle, but she pushed through it, forcing her body to obey her will.

Slowly, with immense effort, Mara tried to lift her upper body, using her weakened arms to prop herself up.
The world around her swam in and out of focus, but she was determined to see where she was.
She looked around, taking in her surroundings: a shiny, new army cot beneath her, stark and cold.
The room was a featureless concrete box, with no windows, just a single metal door,
and two harsh fluorescent lights buzzing above.

Sonia knelt beside her, her presence both comforting and unsettling.
Sonia was dressed in a bathrobe, her face bare and vulnerable, her hands and feet exposed as well.
Her hair was pulled back into a makeshift braid, a far cry from the elegant styles she usually wore.
In her hands, Sonia held another bathrobe, clearly meant for Mara.

“We’ve been kidnapped,” Sonia said quietly, her voice steady but laced with fear.
There was no need for further explanation; the stark reality was all too clear.

With painstaking care, Mara tried to sit up, her body trembling from the effort.
Every muscle protested, but she fought through the pain, her determination stronger than the agony she felt.
She needed to understand what had happened, where they were, and how they could possibly escape this nightmare.
The rough texture of the army cot pressed into her skin as she pushed herself upright,
her body shaking with the exertion.

Sonia moved closer, ready to help if needed, but also aware of Mara's fierce independence.
Mara managed to sit, her breath coming in shallow gasps.
The pain was overwhelming, but she refused to give in to it.
Her head drooped, her neck muscles too weak to hold it upright.
Her back ached with every breath, the absence of her supportive shell making every movement a struggle.
Sonia moved quickly to help,
guiding Mara with gentle hands toward the metal toilet mounted against the side of their cell.
Mara could barely walk, her feet refusing to flatten after years spent in an en-pointe position,
her body refusing to cooperate with even the simplest actions.

When they reached the toilet, Mara collapsed onto it, all pretense of dignity gone.
She felt utterly exposed and helpless, her body betraying her as she fought to regain control over her leaking bowels.
The effort was agonizing, each movement sending fresh waves of pain through her already weakened muscles.
Sitting there, she tried to clean herself as best she could,
the rough washcloths and cold water a poor substitute for the cleanliness she was used to.
But she couldn’t stand, so she did everything from where she sat, her mind reeling from the indignity of it all.

Sonia, also struggling with her own weakened body, handed Mara a bathrobe.
Mara accepted it gratefully, covering her bare, vulnerable body with the soft fabric.
It was the first bit of comfort she’d felt since waking up in this nightmare,
and she couldn’t stop the tears that began to fall silently down her cheeks.
She had been stripped of everything that made her feel safe, and now she was left with nothing but pain and despair.

A small flap in the metal door opened with a harsh clang, and a steel tray was pushed into the room.
On it was a simple meal and a bottle of water for each of them.
The sight of solid food filled Mara with dread.
She had lived on liquid nourishment for so long
that the idea of chewing and swallowing real food was almost alien to her.
The smells, the textures—everything was overwhelming,
and she felt a wave of nausea rise up as she forced herself to take a bite.
She gagged, barely able to keep the food down, and stayed close to the toilet in case she lost the battle.

Along with the meal, there were fresh linens for the cots.
Sonia, moving with the same cautious steps as Mara, managed to change the bedding.
The task took longer than it should have, both women struggling with their weakened bodies.
But eventually, Sonia succeeded, making the beds as comfortable as possible in the cold, sterile room.

Mara’s stomach revolted against the unfamiliar food, and she spent what felt like hours on the toilet,
her body purging itself violently.
Sonia stayed by her side, offering comfort where she could,
even as Mara managed to whisper a few strained words that were a poor imitation of speech.
Her hair hung in greasy strands down to her waist,
but she was too weak to care for it, too weak to even leave the toilet.
Sonia moved Mara’s cot closer so she could rest within reach of the toilet in case of another emergency.

That night was unbearable.
Exhaustion weighed heavily on Mara, but sleep was out of reach.
Pain pulsed through every inch of her body, and each movement reminded her of her weakness.
Despite it all, she forced herself to keep going, to push through the agony.
She did simple exercises, flexing her fingers, toes, and limbs, trying to regain some strength.
She took small bites of food, determined to keep something down, to give her body the energy it desperately needed.

The days blurred together in a fog of pain, sleep, and boredom.
Time lost meaning as Mara struggled to adjust to her new, terrifying reality.
At some point, Sonia made a ruckus, shouting at their unseen captors through the door,
demanding assistance, clothing, and shoes.
Her voice echoed in the small, featureless cell, filled with frustration and desperation.
She explained that they couldn’t walk properly without some kind of heel, emphasizing their physical limitations.
For days, they had been left with little more than the bare essentials for survival,
and their situation was becoming unbearable.

To their surprise, food continued to arrive regularly, pushed through the flap in the door.
After one particularly loud outburst from Sonia, something else was delivered.
The next tray included two bright orange ballet leotards and a pair of medium-heeled pumps for each of them.
The shoes weren’t a perfect fit, but they were better than going barefoot.
Despite the pain, Mara’s memory of her ballet training pushed her forward.
She put on the leotard and shoes, feeling the unfamiliar fabric against her skin, and forced herself to move.
The pain was intense, every step a reminder of how weak she had become.
Her shortened tendons protested, and the weight of her body on unfamiliar points in her feet was excruciating.
But she pressed on, determined to regain some control over her body.

The food, though still unappealing, became easier to tolerate.
Mara forced herself to eat her share and keep it down, knowing she needed the strength.
Each day, she pushed herself a little harder, trying to rebuild the endurance she had once taken for granted.


Mara trained her voice, which she had not used for several years.
She pushed herself to move from a whisper to modulated, if quiet, speech, even though she hated every second of it.
She supplemented her speech with hand-dancing the way little girls do before receiving their first mask,
using the familiar motions to convey the nuances and emotions that her voice couldn’t carry.
Sonia supported her through it all, sensing how close Mara was to breaking under the weight of her trauma.
She knew that Mara needed to feel some semblance of control, and speaking was one way to reclaim that.

While exploring the cell early on, Sonia and Mara found a pile of objects in the corner, covered by a rough blanket.
Beneath it, they discovered the broken remains of Mara’s iGuardian, Sonia’s chastity belt,
and the shredded remains of the clothing they had worn to the conference.
Mara did not understand why their unseen captors left the pieces here with them.
In a hoarse, barely understandable voice, and at the same time with clumsy, half-remembered hand-dancing, she asked Sonia, "Are they trying to mock us with that? It's depressing, seeing my iGuardian destroyed. I feel so exposed and helpless."
The device had been her shield, her prison, and now it lay in ruins before her.

As they sifted through the debris of their past lives,
Sonia found some salvageable electronics and batteries among the wreckage of Mara’s iGuardian.
With quiet determination, she began to wire something together, working with whatever scraps she could find.
Mara watched her with disbelief, clinging to the idea that Sonia might be able to get them out of this nightmare.

At night, when the harsh fluorescent lights finally dimmed, Sonia put her plan into action.
Using the makeshift tools she had fashioned from the remnants of their gear,
she tampered with the electronic lock on their cell door.
The process was slow and nerve-wracking, every small sound amplified in the stillness of the night.
The final piece, a shard of titanium from Mara’s vaginal shield, proved to be the key.
With a quiet click, the door’s lock disengaged.

Dressed in their tattered white bathrobes, bright orange leotards, and ill-fitting heels,
the two women slipped out of their cell and into the concrete hallway beyond.
The corridor was silent, cold, and unforgiving, but it was a form of freedom.

Sonia hadn’t really expected them to make it out.
Her main goal was to get as close to the open sky as possible, just in case they could send out a signal.
Tucked away in the pocket of her bathrobe was the GPS transmitter from Mara's iGuardian,
now wired to some salvaged batteries.
The small device was put together in a makeshift way, and its “GPS lock” LED blinked with a slow red pulse,
indicating it had no idea where it was,
while the “net” LED flashed similarly, unable to connect to any base station to update its location.

With their hard heels clicking on the concrete, the women moved as quietly as they could through the maze of corridors,
passing several numbered metal doors identical to their own cell.
The dim lighting made the space feel even more oppressive, and every sound seemed to echo in the silence.

At the end of the corridor, a metal-barred door blocked their way to a set of concrete stairs leading upwards.
Sonia crouched down and started working on the lock with her improvised titanium tool,
her breath slow and steady as she concentrated.
Mara, meanwhile, explored the small area around them, her eyes scanning for anything that might help.

In a nearby guard room, she found a desk and a cot.
Her hands felt along the underside of the desk, searching the drawers and crevices for anything hidden.
When she reached up to the underside of the second-lowest drawer, her fingers brushed against something sticky—tape.
She carefully peeled it back and found a key attached.
With a small, silent gasp of excitement, she slipped it free and hurried back to Sonia,
holding it out to her with a hopeful look.

Sonia took the key with a silent nod and inserted it into the lock.
The metal door swung open with a quiet creak,
and the two women slowly ascended the stairs, each step taking them closer to the unknown.

At the top of the stairs, they found a final door, unlocked.
When they pushed it open, they were met with the cold, crisp air of the high Alps.
The night sky stretched above them, the stars glimmering like scattered diamonds.
They were standing in the inner yard of a lone mountain farmstead, far from any sign of civilization.
The chill wind bit at their skin, and they huddled closer together,
their thin bathrobes and leotards offering little protection from the cold.

Mara asked with her hands in the cold air, *What now? We cannot stay here.*

Sonia didn’t answer right away.
She focused on the LEDs on the GPS device, hoping they would turn green.
One by one, the red blinking lights shifted to a steady green,
signaling that the device had finally locked onto their position and connected to a network.

Sonia pointed at the LEDs with a small smile, and Mara’s face lit up with relief.
She clapped her hands softly, her excitement muted but genuine.
They had done it.
The signal was out.

But Sonia knew they couldn’t stay.
The bitter cold was seeping into their bones, and their thin clothes offered no protection against the alpine night.
"Let’s go back," she whispered.
"If we’re lucky, they haven’t noticed we’ve been out.
It’s time for our guardians and the grenadiers to take care of us."

Mara nodded, understanding that they weren’t meant to be the heroes in this story,
but literally the damsels in distress.
Slowly, they retraced their steps, locking the doors behind them.
Mara carefully taped the key back to its hiding place, ensuring nothing was out of place.

Finally, they slipped back into their cell,
the concrete walls feeling even more suffocating after their brief taste of freedom.
They crept into their hard cots, the cold metal pressing against their skin,
and waited in the dark, hoping help would arrive soon.

The rescue was swift and chaotic.
Down in their concrete cell, Mara and Sonia listened to the sounds above: the thrum of helicopters,
the sharp crack of gunshots, the shouts of men, and the deep, resonating boom of an explosion.
The very walls that had imprisoned them now served as a bunker, shielding them from the battle raging above.

Heavy footsteps echoed through the corridor, followed by the shouts of soldiers.
"Secured!"
"Two here!"
"One injured!"
The clanging of doors, the sound of crying—it was a chaotic symphony of liberation.
Finally, their cell door swung open, and heavily armored grenadiers stormed in.
"Secured!" one shouted, and another took stock of the room, 
"Two more!
Uninjured!"

After the grenadiers, medics entered, carrying supplies.
Their faces were focused as they moved to care for Sonia and Mara.
Mara, too exhausted and overwhelmed to fully process what was happening,
was secured to a stretcher and injected with a sedative that quickly dulled her senses.

Her next memory was soft and familiar—lying in her bed at home.
The warmth of the blanket, the gentle comfort of the bed—it all felt almost unreal.
She was unbound, still naked under the blanket, but there was no cold, no harshness.
As her eyes adjusted to the dim light of the room, she saw Philippe sitting beside her, looking exhausted and worn.

Before she could fully wake up, Philippe was at her side, cradling her in his arms.
His embrace was firm and desperate, as though he feared letting go might cause her to vanish.
Mara felt the trembling in his body, the raw emotion he had been holding back.

They both began to cry, overwhelmed by sadness, relief, fear, and trauma.
Mara felt the tightness in her chest loosen just a bit, knowing she was safe, that Philippe was there.
The physical pain of the past few days melted away, leaving only the rawness of her emotions.

"I'm here, Mara," Philippe whispered through his tears. "You're safe now. You're home."

Mara clung to him, her voice still weak but filled with the same depth of emotion. "Philippe, I was so scared. I—"

"Shh," he soothed, holding her close.
"You're safe.
Sonia is safe.
Anna wasn't kidnapped, she escaped with a few scratches.
I'm here.
I'll stay with you.
We’ll get through this together."

Mara cuddled into Philippe's arms, her body still trembling from the overwhelming emotions.
She knew she needed to tell him everything, but the words felt foreign on her tongue after so long.
“The attack... it happened so fast,” she began, her voice hoarse and barely above a whisper.
“One moment I was looking at the feed bag, the next... the door just slammed into me.
It was... violent.
But the iGuardian, the mask... they protected me from the worst of it.”

She paused, her hands moving with a different rhythm, one that spoke of fear and vulnerability.
"But then... when I woke up...
I was without my shell, Philippe.
I was completely vulnerable.
I could feel... everything.
My skin, the air, the rough blanket against me.
It was too much.
I felt... exposed, like I was nothing."

Philippe’s grip tightened around her, offering silent support as she continued.

“I felt filthy,” she said, her voice cracking as she forced the words out.
“The belt... it always kept me clean.
I never had to worry about anything like that.
But there, without it...
I was leaking, I couldn’t control my body... it was disgusting.”

Her hands danced, mirroring the depth of her shame and helplessness.
"I felt so dirty, so... lost.
I couldn't even stand properly.
My neck, my back... they hurt so much without the support.
I couldn’t even hold my head up.
I felt... broken."

She took a shaky breath, trying to steady herself as she moved on to the next part.
“And the food...
I couldn’t stand it.
The feed bags are so smooth, so easy.
But this... this was raw, intense.
The tastes were overwhelming.
I almost couldn’t keep it down.
It was like... like I was being forced to go back to something primitive, something... uncivilized.”

Her hands stilled, and she looked up at Philippe, her eyes pleading.
"I need the belt, Philippe.
I can’t...
I don’t want to be without it.
I need that shell, that protection.
I want to be... civilized again.
Please."

Philippe gently stroked her hair, his voice soft but firm.
"We'll get you back into an iGuardian, Mara.
I promise.
You won't have to go through that again.
You're safe now, and I'll make sure you're cared for."

Mara nodded, her hands expressing her relief. "Thank you, Philippe. I just... I want to feel whole again."

Philippe kissed her forehead, holding her close as they both began to calm.
