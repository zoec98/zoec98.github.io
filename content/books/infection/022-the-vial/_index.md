# The Vial

At the lakeside café, Sonia and Anna met, both dressed in the elegant style of the Changed.
Each woman was completely covered, with a hijab, mask and gloves.
Sitting bolt upright at their table,
facing each other and elegantly signing back and forth they epitomized the new world Dr. Cheng had created.

Anna began, her hands crafting each sign with precision. "Sonia,
the analysis of the genome you procured has been enlightening.
The virus extends far beyond just altering gender dynamics.
It's engineered
to combat climate change and the detrimental aspects of late-stage capitalism
by fostering a deep sense of interconnectedness and mutual dependency among all individuals."

She continued, elaborating on the specific behavioral modifications encoded by the virus.
"One key adjustment encourages a significant reduction in consumption.
This isn't merely about promoting recycling or modest consumption but about a profound shift towards minimalism and sustainability in daily life."

Anna's hands moved gracefully, almost painting the air with her words.
"Another modification targets economic behaviors.
It instills a collective drive to dismantle hyperwealth.
This includes support for progressive taxation, rigorous compliance checks on corporate activities,
and a communal effort to regulate industries that harm the environment."

Her gestures grew more animated as she delved deeper.
"Furthermore, the virus promotes a restructuring of social norms to prioritize communal well-being over individual gain.
This extends into urban planning and energy usage,
pushing communities to adopt green technologies and sustainable practices naturally."

Sonia, observing intently, responded with a casual tone that contrasted with Anna's formality.
"So, it's like Dr. Cheng is programming society to fix itself?
Rewiring people to naturally fight against the very things screwing up our planet and our societies?"

Anna nodded affirmatively, signing, "precisely, Sonia.
The virus isn't just changing how we interact; it's reshaping our priorities and our very motivations.
By enhancing our interdependence,
it makes us more inclined to act in ways that benefit not just individuals but the entire community."

Sonia's curiosity was piqued yet she was conflicted "That's… kind of revolutionary," she replied, "but also terrifying?
To think that compliance isn't just encouraged but literally built into our DNA now."

Anna's expression softened, a gentle reminder of her acceptance.
"It is a shift, Sonia, but one that could lead to a more harmonious and sustainable world.
Think of it as evolution, not just at a biological level but at a societal level too.
The virus challenges us to transcend our limitations and embrace a greater purpose.

"There is more," Anna continued,
her  hands moving with deliberate precision
as she explained the nuances of Sonia's unique condition through the sophisticated ballet of hand-dancing.
"This section here," she pointed on her laptop screen,
detailing the genetic markers, "explains why the virus didn't fully control you, Sonia.
It targets a specific protein, but in your case, it attached to something similar but not identical.
That's why you didn't experience the compulsions like others.

"You possess all the knowledge imprinted by the virus,
the memories and values, but without the compulsion to serve or follow.
It's an anomaly," Anna summarized, her signs meticulous and her expression earnest.

After a pause, Anna offered gently, with her hands shaping each word carefully, "I could fix you.
Activate the compulsion.
It will bring you peace, heal you from this… conflict you carry."

Sonia watched Anna's hands, feeling the weight of the proposition.
Her response came hesitantly as her hand-dancing reflected her inner unease, her movements less fluid than Anna's.
"Fix me?
Like I'm broken?" she signed, her brows furrowing slightly.
"That peace… it'd be real peace, or just me giving up fighting?
Because that's not peace, Anna, that's surrender."

Anna's reply was patient, her hands forming each sign with empathetic precision.
"Sonia, our society values living together in harmony and creating a web of dependencies between people.
With the compulsion activated, you would feel what we feel.
The satisfaction of fulfilling your role, the absence of conflict within.
This is not surrender.
It is working with your role, not against it."

Sonia shook her head, her hands expressing her turmoil.
"But my role… isn't that something I should choose, not something a virus, or anyone else, chooses for me?"
Her gestures were rapid, a stark contrast to Anna's composed signing.
"I value my ability to choose, Anna.
Maybe more than I value the peace you're talking about."

Anna nodded, acknowledging Sonia's resistance.
"It's your choice, Sonia.
I only offer the solution because I see the struggle you endure.
The virus designed our society to eliminate such internal conflicts.
You are an exception, living with a foot in two worlds.
I could bring you peace."

Sonia signed back slowly, her decision clear in her deliberate movements.
"I think… I need to keep struggling, Anna.
Maybe find my own kind of peace, one that doesn't require me to change what fundamentally makes me, me."

Anna took out a small glass vial containing a clear liquid from her bag.
"Even so, I must give you this," she offered, handing the vial to Sonia.
"It's something to 'fix' you if you ever decide you're ready to accept the peace of the Changed.
Simply swallow it, pure or with water, and within two hours, you will be healed."

Sonia hesitated but took the vial and placed it in her bag.
"I… I'll keep it," she communicated through her gestures, although the idea unsettled her.
They fell silent, watching as women wearing the new style of masks walked by.
