                                                                                                                                                  Chapter 4: Anna is Changed

                                                                                                                                                  Anna is Changed#

                                                                                                                                                  Three grueling days later, Anna began to sense a shift; the worst of her crisis had passed on Wednesday night. Exhausted, dehydrated, and ravenous, she found herself mechanically eating and drinking in the kitchen, leaving a chaotic trail of crumbs and puddles behind her. It felt as if she was emerging from the ordeal. Or so she thought. Her memory of those days was fragmented, almost wiped clean.

                                                                                                                                                  However, she vividly recalled the feeling that surged through her when she saw the mess she had left behind—a feeling all too familiar from her dreams. Disorder was intolerable; a woman must always be neat, tidy, clean. Compelled by an overwhelming impulse, she began to clean up. The moment she complied, a deep sense of satisfaction and reward filled her. Cleaning felt right, tidiness was good.

                                                                                                                                                  After restoring order, Anna reached for her phone. It was early Thursday morning. Struggling to compose herself, she dialed the lab, her voice barely recognizable even to her own ears. “I am still alive, but sick. Just a cold,” she managed to say, though she knew it was far more serious than that.

                                                                                                                                                  First, she needed a shower. She felt overwhelmingly dirty, and as this realization dawned, a creeping dread began to envelop her. It started as a faint fog, like a haze dimming the brightness of a sunny day, gradually intensifying. Once she yielded to the urge to cleanse herself, the familiar reward surged through her again, confirming her compliance. This cycle of conditioned responses and involuntary actions haunted Anna, the horror of losing control over her own will manifesting in the most mundane aspects of her life.

                                                                                                                                                  As Anna stepped into the bathroom, the reflection staring back at her was both familiar and deeply unsettling. Her face, marked by dark circles and a sickly pallor, was expected after her ordeal. However, the transformation of her body filled her with dread. She looked like an exaggerated, Barbie-like caricature of herself. Starting at the neck, her body was completely hairless, and the bathroom floor was littered with what used to be her body fuzz. This stark reality confirmed it was no hallucination: all her body hair—legs, arms, armpits, and pubic area—was gone, leaving her skin unnaturally smooth and pearly. Unblemished. Not a single hair anywhere from the neck down. Above her neck, her hair had undergone a drastic change, growing thick and strong, cascading down to her waist in a vibrant chestnut brown, straightened by its own weight, devoid of any natural curls. Her physique had also altered dramatically. Her waist was now unnaturally slim, almost comically so, while her breasts had enlarged significantly, appearing even larger due to her reduced waistline. Although they were no longer painful to touch, they remained hypersensitive.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna felt a profound sense of violation and dysphoria. Her body had been transformed into a male gaze fantasy—a grotesque embodiment of what someone else, presumably a man, believed a woman should resemble. Reshaped by a virus engineered to turn her into a living masturbatory fantasy. As she felt her rage begin to assemble and rise in her, something even more horrifying than her physical transformation happened. An alien inner voice urged her to embrace her new appearance. It was clearly not her own voice, but it was coming from within her mind, sounding a lot like her. “You’re becoming pretty,” it cooed. Alongside this voice, waves of foreign emotions attempted to push her feelings of dysphoria and horror to the margins, overwriting them with enthusiasm, joy, and pride about being “pretty”. Memories of past insecurities about her body resurfaced, only to be reinterpreted by this intrusive voice, which suggested that all her previous flaws had been ‘corrected.’

                                                                                                                                                  This voice persistently eroded her resistance, insinuating that these changes were improvements, that being more ‘feminine’ was inherently better. Her very sense of self was under siege, slowly being reshaped by an unseen, insidious force. Horror and disgust washed to the surface, to be pushed away immediately by pride, joy and arousal on watching her ‘improved’ shape.

                                                                                                                                                  Her body and mind had been reshaped into a form she never desired, manipulated by the voice that seemed to be quickly becoming indistinguishable from her own. This voice, like the virus itself, was relentless in its pursuit to overwrite her genuine feelings, coaxing her to not only accept but embrace the changes inflicted upon her. Leaning into that acceptance offered a disturbing sense of relief. Again, she found herself inexplicably feeling good, feeling sexy, and feeling sexy made her wet again.

                                                                                                                                                  Anna quickly caught herself. Those thoughts weren’t truly hers; they were the voice’s, now mimicking her own so closely that it was becoming difficult to distinguish it as alien. Resisting these intrusive thoughts drained her of the little energy she had mustered post-illness. The notion of being molded into someone else’s ideal of “pretty” revolted her. It was a profound betrayal of her values, a stark violation of her autonomy. The compliments in her mind on her new appearance felt like mockery.

                                                                                                                                                  They also felt good. She was possibly recovering from the fever, but her fight against the virus was far from over. It was still battling to dominate her mind, pushing her to conform to and be content with these unsettling transformations. And yet, the pleasure it offered was seductive. It felt good. So good. She yearned to feel good, to escape the illness, to feel complete once again. “This is what a proper woman looks like,” the voice helpfully suggested, infusing her with a false sense of acceptance and pride that felt almost genuine. Anna found herself questioning her very essence: What had she become, and what exactly defined a “proper woman”? She no longer trusted her thoughts or emotions. Every sentiment, every idea, had to be scrutinized: Was this her true self, or merely an imprint left by the virus’s manipulation? Every feeling, every thought needed to be checked: Was that her real self, or an injection by whatever the virus did to her?
